## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 62 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Restoration

This chapter prophesies concerning a future time of peace and restoration. It is unlikely to have been fulfilled in Isaiah’s day. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/restore]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/fulfill]])

---

## Isaiah 62:1

**Quote:** For Zion’s sake I will not be silent, and for Jerusalem’s sake I will not be quiet
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
Both of these statements mean the same thing. Here “Zion” and “Jerusalem” both represent the people who live in Jerusalem. Alternate translation: [For the sake of the people of Jerusalem I will not be silent] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** I will not be silent
It is most likely that “I” refers to Isaiah.

**Quote:** until her righteousness proceeds brightly, and her salvation as a burning torch
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
Both clauses reassure the people that God will eventually come and save the people of Israel and that it will be as apparent as light is. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism]])

---

## Isaiah 62:3

**Quote:** You will also be a crown of beauty in the hand of Yahweh, and a turban of kingship in the hand of your God
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These mean the same thing and are combined for emphasis. They say that Jerusalem will become a royal city under the power and authority of God. Yahweh’s hand is a metonym for his power and authority. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

---

## Isaiah 62:4

**Quote:** It will no more … will it be said, “Desolate.”
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These mean the same thing are combined for emphasis.

**Quote:** It will no more be said of you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [People will no longer say about you]

**Quote:** nor of your land any longer will it be said
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [nor will people any longer say about your land]

**Quote:** your land will be married
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This means Yahweh will love the people of Israel and will always be with them as is a husband.

---

## Isaiah 62:5

**Quote:** as a young man marries a young woman, so your sons will marry you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
Here “sons” refers to the people of Israel and “you” refers to Judah, the land of Israel. This means the people will take possession of the land as a man takes possession of his young wife.

**Quote:** as a bridegroom rejoices over his bride, your God will rejoice over you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This emphasizes God’s happiness about his relationship with his people.

---

## Isaiah 62:6

**Quote:** I have put
Here “I” may refer to Isaiah or to Yahweh.

**Quote:** watchmen on your walls
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This refers to prophets, officials, or possibly angels, who are constantly praying for the people of Jerusalem like watchmen who constantly guard the city. See how you translated “watchmen” in [Isaiah 52:8](../52/08.md) and [Isaiah 56:10](../56/10.md).

**Quote:** they are not silent day or night
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-litotes
This means they are continually pleading to Yahweh or calling to each other. Alternate translation: [they are earnestly praying to Yahweh throughout the entire day] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism]])

## Isaiah 62:7

**Quote:** Do not allow him to rest
Here “him” refers to Yahweh.

## Isaiah 62:8

**Quote:** by his right hand and by the arm of his strength
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The right hand and arm represent power and authority. Alternate translation: [by his power and authority]

**Quote:** Surely I will no longer give your grain as food for your enemies
This means Yahweh will not let enemies conquer the people of Israel and take their grain anymore. Perhaps enemies took the grain in the past as a tax or to feed their own armies.

**Quote:** I will no longer give your grain as food for your enemies … Foreigners will not drink your new wine
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These statements are set together for emphasis and completion.

---

## Isaiah 62:9

**Quote:** those who harvest the grain … those who pick the grapes
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These statements are set together for emphasis and completion.

## Isaiah 62:10

**Quote:** Come through, come through the gates
The phrase “come through” is repeated to show urgency.

**Quote:** Build it, build the highway
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The word “build” is repeated to emphasize that Yahweh urgently wants the road prepared. The “highway” represents the way the people can return. This is similar to [Isaiah 40:3](../40/03.md) and [Isaiah 57:14](../57/14.md).

**Quote:** Gather out the stones
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
“Take the stones out of the road to make it smooth.” Stones represent all the obstacles to quick travel.

**Quote:** Raise up a signal flag for the nations
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
A signal flag represents something to draw the attention of others. This means Yahweh is calling the people of the other nations to take notice of the land of Israel and to see what Yahweh has accomplished as he said he would.

## Isaiah 62:11

**Quote:** Look
Alternate translation: [Take notice!]

**Quote:** the ends of the earth
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The places on the earth that are very far away are spoken of as if they were the places where the earth ends. This phrase also forms a merism and refers to everywhere in between the ends. See how you translated this in [Isaiah 40:28](../40/28.md). AT “the farthest places of the earth” or “the entire earth” (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism]])

**Quote:** the daughter of Zion
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
“Daughter” represents the people of Jerusalem (Zion). See how you translated this in [Isaiah 1:8](../01/08.md).

**Quote:** See, his reward is with him, and his recompense is going before him
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These clauses represent the same idea for emphasis. See how you translated a similar phrase in [Isaiah 40:10](../40/10.md).
