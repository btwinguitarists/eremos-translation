## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 58 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Fasting

The practice of fasting became more common after the exile. This was intended to bring people into a right relationship with God by creating a designated time to concentrate on him. It may have been believed that Yahweh heard people better when they fasted. The significance of fasting is derived from the faster’s attitude of repentance and faith in Yahweh and not the practice itself. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/repent]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/faith]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### “Yet they seek me daily”

The people did not truly seek Yahweh every day. They practiced the outward aspects of the Jewish religion, but their hearts were far from Yahweh. (See: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

## Isaiah 58:1

**Quote:** Lift up your voice like a trumpet
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This means shout loudly. Here “your” refers to Isaiah. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-you]])

**Quote:** Confront my people with their rebellion, and the house of Jacob with their sins
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean basically the same thing. Together they strengthen the urgency to confront Yahweh’s people.

---

## Isaiah 58:3

**Quote:** ‘Why have we fasted,’ they say, ‘but you do not see it? Why have we humbled ourselves, but you do not notice?’
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
The people of Israel use questions to complain to God because they feel he is ignoring them.

---

## Isaiah 58:4

**Quote:** Look
“Behold!” or “Pay attention.” Yahweh confronts their questioning by telling them to pay attention.

**Quote:** fist of wickedness
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
“wicked fist.” This shows that they fight viciously. “Fist” represents anger that is physically violent.

---

## Isaiah 58:5

**Quote:** Is this really the kind of fast that I would want … under himself?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses a question to scold the people. They act like they are humble before God, but they are hurting other people. Alternate translation: [This is not the kind of fast I want … under himself.]

**Quote:** A day for anybody to humble himself, for him to bow down his head like a reed
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This means the person is bowing down, but he is not truly humble. “A reed” represents a weak plant that bends easily.

**Quote:** Do you really call this a fast, a day that pleases Yahweh?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses a question to scold the people. Alternate translation: [Surely you do not think this kind of fast pleases me!]

---

## Isaiah 58:6

**Quote:** Is not this the fast that I choose … break every yoke?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses a question to teach the people about the kind of fast that pleases him. Alternate translation: [This is the fast that I choose … break every yoke.]

**Quote:** To release wicked bonds, to undo the ropes of the yoke, to set the crushed ones free, and to break every yoke
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
All of these phrases mean they should help those people whom the wicked are hurting and oppressing. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism]])

---

## Isaiah 58:7

**Quote:** Is it not to … your house?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses another question to teach the people. Alternate translation: [It is to … your house.]

**Quote:** break your bread
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
This phrase represents starting the meal by the host tearing the loaf of bread apart.

---

## Isaiah 58:8

**Quote:** Then your light would be broken open like the sunrise
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
“Break open” here is an idiom that means when a light begins to shine brightly. This means that if they help people in need, their deeds will be like lights others will see, as when the sun comes up after a dark night. Or the light may refer to the light of the Lord, shining on them and blessing them. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

**Quote:** your healing would quickly sprout up
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This means God will bless and restore them quickly, as a wound heals quickly.

**Quote:** your righteousness would go before you, and the glory of Yahweh would be your rearguard
This refers to the time when God protected the people of Israel as they escaped from Egypt. This means God will again protect them from their enemies if they will do what is right.

**Quote:** your righteousness
This expression in Hebrew can also be interpreted as the one who gives righteousness or even victory. In this context, this expression would refer to God. A few modern versions have this interpretation.

---

## Isaiah 58:10

**Quote:** then your light will rise in the darkness, and your darkness will be like the noonday
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
“Your light” represents the acts of kindness that will be examples to everyone, and the “darkness,” the bad deeds, will be overcome by their good deeds.

---

## Isaiah 58:11

**Quote:** satisfy you in regions where there is no water
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
“Water” represents all they need for an abundant life even when their surroundings do not have enough.

**Quote:** You will be like a watered garden
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
“A watered garden” represents abundance and plenty so they will have all they need.

**Quote:** like a spring of water, whose waters never fail
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
“A spring of water” represents a source of abundance in a land where water is precious.

---

## Isaiah 58:12

**Quote:** you will be called
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [people will call you]

---

## Isaiah 58:13

**Quote:** you turn back your feet from traveling on the Sabbath day, and from doing your own pleasure on my holy day
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Here the people are represented by their “feet.” God did not allow long travels or work on the day of rest. Alternate translation: [you stop traveling and doing what you want to do on the Sabbath, my holy day]

---

## Isaiah 58:14

**Quote:** I will make you ride on the heights of the earth
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This refers to God exalting the nation in reputation and power in response to living righteously.

**Quote:** for the mouth of Yahweh has spoken
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
The word “mouth” symbolizes what Yahweh says. Alternate translation: [for Yahweh has spoken it]
