## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 56 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Gentiles

Many scholars believe this chapter prophesies that Gentiles will be able to come to a saving faith in Yahweh. At this time, the Israelites believed that only an Israelite could be saved. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/save]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/faith]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Beasts

The Israelites are compared to beasts and dogs in this chapter. This would have been very offensive to them. (See: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

## Isaiah 56:2

**Quote:** who holds it tightly
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
Here “holds it tightly” is an idiom that means to continue to observe something. Alternate translation: [who is careful to always do this]

**Quote:** keeps his hand from doing any evil
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Here the “hand” represents the whole person and emphasizes the person’s actions or behaviors. Alternate translation: [does not do evil things]

---

## Isaiah 56:3

**Quote:** See, I am a dry tree
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This means eunuchs may have thought they could not be a part of God’s people because they were deformed by castration (and for this reason could not have children). Israelites did not practice castration; foreigners did, sometimes for punishment. Eunuchs who had accepted the Hebrew faith knew that normally they were not allowed to worship in the temple (Deut. [23:1](../23/01.md)). The full meaning of this statement can be made clear. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

## Isaiah 56:9

**Quote:** All you wild beasts of the field, come and devour, all you beasts in the forest
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
God is calling armies of other nations by comparing them to animals to come and attack the people of Israel.

---

## Isaiah 56:10

**Quote:** All their watchmen are blind
This means Israel’s leaders cannot or perhaps do not want to see what is going on in society.

**Quote:** They are all silent dogs
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The leaders are supposed to open their mouths and warn the people, but they do not. Comparing someone to a dog in this society is a great insult.

**Quote:** They dream, and lying down they love to sleep
This means the leaders are not bringing God’s word of warning to Israel but prefer their own comfort.

## Isaiah 56:11

**Quote:** The dogs have big appetites
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh continues comparing Israel’s bad leaders to dogs.
