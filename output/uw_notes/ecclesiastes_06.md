## Chapter intro

# Ecclesiastes 6 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in 6:7–11.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Satisfaction

While a person may be given a great many things, they are worthless and provide no sense of satisfaction or peace. It is assumed that only Yahweh can provide these things to man. Solomon is depressed that he had everything he could have ever wanted in life, but they were not enough to give him satisfaction or peace. (See: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

## Ecclesiastes 6:3

**Quote:** fathers a hundred children
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-numbers
“fathers 100 children.” This is a hypothetical situation. This is also an exaggeration and is applicable to people with fewer than 100 children. Alternate translation: [fathers many children] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-hypo]] and [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-hyperbole]])

**Quote:** lives many years, so that the days of his years are many
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean basically the same thing and are combined for emphasis. Alternate translation: [lives many years]

**Quote:** his heart is not satisfied with good
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
This refers to a man by his “heart” to emphasize his feelings. Alternate translation: [he is not content with good things]

**Quote:** he is not buried
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. This could mean: (1) “no one buries him at all” or (2) he receives no honor, “no one buries him properly.” (See also: or [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

## Ecclesiastes 6:8

**Quote:** what advantage has the wise person over the fool?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
The author uses this rhetorical question to emphasize that a wise person does not have any more lasting benefits than a fool. If it would be helpful in your language, you could express this question as a statement. Alternate translation: [it seems the wise person has no advantage over the fool.]

**Quote:** What advantage does the poor man have even if he knows how to act in front of other people?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
The author uses this rhetorical question to emphasize that a poor man does not have any more lasting benefits than someone else. If it would be helpful in your language, you could express this question as a statement. Alternate translation: [The poor man has no advantage even if he knows how to act in front of other people.]

## Ecclesiastes 6:12

**Quote:** For who knows what is good for man … he passes like a shadow?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
The author uses this rhetorical question to emphasize that no person truly knows what is good for man. If it would be helpful in your language, you could express this question as a statement. Alternate translation: [No one knows what is good for man … he passes like a shadow.]

**Quote:** in his life during his futile, numbered days through which he passes like a shadow
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This speaks of how life passes quickly by saying that it is like a shadow that quickly disappears. The phrase “numbered days” emphasizes that a person’s life is short. Alternate translation: [during his futile, short life, which he passes through as quickly as a shadow passes by]

**Quote:** Who can tell a man … after he passes?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
The author uses this rhetorical question to emphasize that no one knows what will happen after a person dies. If it would be helpful in your language, you could express this question as a statement. Alternate translation: [No one can tell a man … after he passes.]

**Quote:** what will come under the sun
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
This refers to things that are done on earth. See how you translated “under the sun” in [Ecclesiastes 1:3](../01/03.md). Alternate translation: [what will happen on the earth]

**Quote:** after he passes
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-euphemism
This is a polite expression for death. Alternate translation: [after he dies]
