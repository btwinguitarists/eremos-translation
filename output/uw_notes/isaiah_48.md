## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 48 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

### Yahweh and the false gods

This chapter is set up to compare Yahweh with various false Gods whom the Israelites worship. There really is no comparison between the two because Yahweh alone is God. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/falsegod]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Exile

Yahweh explains purpose behind the Jews exile in Babylon, even though it is still in the future. This emphasizes that Yahweh is much more powerful than the false gods.

## Isaiah 48:1

**Quote:** Hear this
“Listen to my message.” Yahweh is speaking.

**Quote:** house of Jacob
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “house” refers to the descendants of Jacob. Alternate translation: [descendants of Jacob]

**Quote:** who are called by the name Israel
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [whom everyone calls the people of Israel]

**Quote:** have come from the sperm of Judah
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This phrase emphasizes that they are the direct, physical descendants of Judah. Alternate translation: [are the descendants of Judah]

**Quote:** invoke the God of Israel
Alternate translation: [call on the God of Israel]

## Isaiah 48:4

**Quote:** your neck muscles tight as iron, and your forehead like bronze
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
Yahweh compares the tightness of their neck muscles and the hardness of their foreheads to the hardness of iron and bronze. Here, to have a tight neck or a hard forehead is a metaphor that means the people are stubborn. Alternate translation: [it is as if your necks were iron and your heads were bronze] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

## Isaiah 48:5

**Quote:** I declared these things to you beforehand; before they happened I informed you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
This is saying the same thing twice for emphasis.

---

## Isaiah 48:6

**Quote:** will you not admit what I said is true?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses a question to scold the people of Israel for not admitting what they should know is true. Alternate translation: [you are stubborn and will not admit what I said is true.]

---

## Isaiah 48:8

**Quote:** these things were not unfolded to your ears beforehand
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of explaining something as if it were unfolding it. The word “ears” represents the people who are listening. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [I did not explain these things to you beforehand] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche]] and [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** from birth
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of the nation’s beginning as if it were its birth.

---

## Isaiah 48:9

**Quote:** For the sake of my name I will defer my anger
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “name” refers to Yahweh’s reputation. Alternate translation: [For the sake of my reputation I will delay my anger]

**Quote:** for my honor I will hold back from destroying you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
This part of the sentence means basically the same thing as the first part.

## Isaiah 48:10

**Quote:** Look, I refined you, but not as silver; I have purified you in the furnace of affliction
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of using affliction to purify his people as if they were precious metals and affliction were a furnace in which he refines them.

---

## Isaiah 48:11

**Quote:** for how can I allow my name to be profaned?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses a question to emphasize that he can never allow his own name to be dishonored. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [for I cannot allow anyone to profane my name.]

---

## Isaiah 48:12

**Quote:** Jacob, and Israel
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
Both of these refer to the people of Israel. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** I am the first, I also am the last
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism
This phrase emphasizes Yahweh’s eternal nature. This could mean: (1) “I am the one who began all things, and I am the one who ends all things” or (2) “I am the one who has always lived, and I am the one who always will live.” See how you translated a similar phrase in [Isaiah 44:6](../44/06.md).

## Isaiah 48:13

**Quote:** my hand laid the foundation of the earth, and my right hand spread out the heavens
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Here “hand” refers to Yahweh. Alternate translation: [I laid the foundation of the earth, and I spread out the heavens]

**Quote:** the foundation of the earth
The word “foundation” normally refers to a stone structure that gives support to a building from underneath. Here it describes a similar structure that was thought to support and hold the earth in place. See how you translated this in [Isaiah 24:18](../24/18.md).

**Quote:** spread out the heavens
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of creating the heavens as if they were fabric which Yahweh stretched out. See how you translated a similar phrase in [Isaiah 42:5](../42/05.md).

**Quote:** when I call to them, they stand up together
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Standing up when Yahweh calls is a metaphor for being ready to obey him. Yahweh speaks of the earth and the heavens as if they were able to hear him and obey him. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification]])

**Quote:** when I call to them
This could mean: (1) “when I call the earth and the heavens” or (2) “when I call the stars in the heavens.”

## Isaiah 48:14

**Quote:** Who among you has announced these things?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses a question to emphasize that the idols have not told them these things. Alternate translation: [None of your idols has told this to you.]

**Quote:** Yahweh’s ally will accomplish his purpose against Babylon. He will carry out Yahweh’s will against the Chaldeans
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
Here “ally” refers to Cyrus. Both of these sentences mean the same thing and are used for emphasis.

**Quote:** his purpose
Alternate translation: [Yahweh’s purpose]

## Isaiah 48:18

**Quote:** If only you had obeyed my commandments
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-hypo
Yahweh describes something that could have happened but did not.

**Quote:** Then your peace and prosperity would have flowed like a river, and your salvation like the waves of the sea
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
These two phrases share similar meanings. In both, Yahweh speaks of Israel’s experiencing abundant blessings as if those blessings flowed like water. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism]])

**Quote:** your salvation like the waves of the sea
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The verb may be supplied from the previous phrase. Alternate translation: [your salvation would have flowed like the waves of the sea]

## Isaiah 48:19

**Quote:** Your descendants would have been as numerous as the sand, and the children from your womb as numerous as the grains of sand
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These both mean that the people would have had more descendants than they could count.

**Quote:** the children from your womb
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of the descendants of the people of Israel as if they were children to which the nation gives birth.

**Quote:** their name would not have been cut off nor blotted out
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The people of Israel being destroyed is spoken of as if their name had been cut off, as one would cut a piece of cloth or cut a branch from a tree, or blotted out. This can be translated in active form. Alternate translation: [I would not have cut off nor blotted out their name] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** their name
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “name” refers to the descendants who would carry on the name of Israel. Alternate translation: [they]

**Quote:** cut off nor blotted out
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
These two expressions in this context refer to destroying the people. Alternate translation: [destroyed]

## Isaiah 48:20

**Quote:** to the ends of the earth
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The places on the earth that are very far away are spoken of as if they were the places where the earth ends. This phrase also forms a merism and refers to everywhere in between the ends. Alternate translation: [to all the farthest places of the earth] or [to the entire earth] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism]])

**Quote:** his servant Jacob
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This refers to the descendants of Jacob. Alternate translation: [the people of Israel, his servants]

---

## Isaiah 48:21

**Quote:** They did not thirst … the waters gushed out
This refers to an event in the history of the people of Israel when Yahweh took care of them while they lived in the desert after escaping Egypt.
