## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 47 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Babylon

This chapter prophesied the destruction of Babylon. At the time that the prophesy was written, Assyria was still in power and Babylon had not yet become a world power. The Chaldeans were not yet a powerful nation either. The people of Judah would become servants or slaves of Babylon and be forced to work very hard. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]])

## Isaiah 47:1

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
# General Information:

In this chapter, Yahweh speaks to Babylon about her downfall as if she were a queen who is humiliated. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification]])

**Quote:** sit in the dust, virgin daughter of Babylon; sit on the ground … daughter of the Chaldeans.
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean basically the same thing. Sitting in the dust was a sign of humiliation. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-symaction]])

**Quote:** virgin daughter of Babylon … daughter of the Chaldeans
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Both of these phrases refer to the city, Babylon, which is spoken of as if it were a daughter. That the city is a “daughter” indicates how people think fondly of her.

**Quote:** without a throne
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “throne” refers to the power to rule. Alternate translation: [without the power to rule]

**Quote:** You will no longer be called dainty and delicate
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [People will no longer call you dainty and delicate]

**Quote:** dainty and delicate
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
These two words share similar meanings. They describe one who is beautiful and lives in luxury. Alternate translation: [very beautiful] or [very luxurious]

## Isaiah 47:14

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh continues to speak to Babylon about her downfall as if she were a queen who is humiliated. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification]])

**Quote:** they will become like stubble. The fire will burn them up
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
Yahweh compares the magicians and sorcerers to straw that burns quickly in a fire. This means that Yahweh will destroy them as easily as fire burns stubble, and so they are powerless to save Babylon.

**Quote:** the hand of the flame
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “hand” represents strength. Alternate translation: [the power of the flame]

**Quote:** There are no coals to warm them and no fire for them to sit by
Yahweh emphasizes that this is a destructive fire by stating that it is not one that people will use to warm themselves.
