## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 17 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

The ULT sets the lines in 17:1–6, 9–14 farther to the right on the page than the rest of the text because they are part of a long quotation.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Damascus

This chapter prophesies destruction against the people of Damascus. Damascus was the capital of Aram. The people of Damascus worship gods other than Yahweh and have fought Yahweh’s people. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]], [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/falsegod]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/peopleofgod]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Ephraim

This chapter prophesied destruction against the kingdom of Israel. They are also called Ephraim in this chapter. Both the northern kingdom of Israel and Aram were conquered at about the same time in history.

## Isaiah 17:2

**Quote:** The cities of Aroer will be abandoned
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. “All the people will abandon the cities of Aroer” (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names]])

**Quote:** no one will frighten them
The word “them” refers to the sheep.

---

## Isaiah 17:3

**Quote:** Fortified cities will disappear from Ephraim
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Ephraim was the largest tribe in Israel. Here it represents the entire northern kingdom of Israel. Alternate translation: [Strong cities will disappear from Israel]

**Quote:** will disappear
This does not mean that they will vanish, but that the cities will be destroyed.

**Quote:** the kingdom from Damascus
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The words “will disappear” are understood from the previous phrase. Damascus was where the king of Aram ruled from. The kingdom disappearing represents the king no longer having royal power. Alternate translation: [the kingdom will disappear from Damascus] or [there will be no royal power in Damascus] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** this is the declaration of Yahweh of hosts
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-123person
Yahweh speaks of himself by name to express the certainty of what he is declaring. Translate as in [Isaiah 14:22](../14/22.md). Alternate translation: [this is what Yahweh of hosts has declared] or [this is what I, Yahweh of hosts, have declared]

---

## Isaiah 17:4

**Quote:** It will come about
This phrase is used here to mark an important event that will happen. If your language has a way for doing this, you could consider using it here.

**Quote:** the glory of Jacob will become thin, and the fatness of his flesh will become lean
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “Jacob” refers to the kingdom of Israel. Israel would no longer be glorious. Rather it would be weak and poor. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

---

## Isaiah 17:5

**Quote:** It will be as when a harvester gathers the standing grain … in the Valley of Rephaim
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
There will be nothing left in the land after God punishes the people of Israel.

**Quote:** the Valley of Rephaim
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names
This is a valley where people normally grew and harvested much food.

---

## Isaiah 17:6

**Quote:** Gleanings will be left
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The word “Gleanings” here represents the people who will still live in Israel. Alternate translation: [But there will be a few people left in Israel]

**Quote:** as when the olive tree is shaken
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
People harvested olive trees by shaking them so that the olives would fall out. Alternate translation: [like the few olives that remain on the olive trees after people harvest them]

**Quote:** four or five
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The word “olives” is understood from the phrase before this. Alternate translation: [four or five olives]

**Quote:** this is the declaration of Yahweh, the God of Israel
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-123person
Yahweh speaks of himself by name to express the certainty of what he is declaring. Alternate translation: [this is what Yahweh, the God of Israel, has declared] or [this is what I, Yahweh, the God of Israel, have declared]

---

## Isaiah 17:7

**Quote:** men will look toward their Maker … will look to the Holy One of Israel
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
Looking toward God here represents hoping that he will help them. Alternate translation: [men will hope that their Maker, the Holy One of Israel, will help them]

**Quote:** men will look
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
The word “men” represents people in general. Alternate translation: [people will look]

**Quote:** their eyes will look
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Here “eyes” represents the people who look. Alternate translation: [they will look] or [the people will look]

**Quote:** Holy One of Israel
See how you translated this in [Isaiah 1:4](../01/04.md).

---

## Isaiah 17:8

**Quote:** They will not look to the altars
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Looking to the altars represents worshiping idols with the hope that the idols will help them. Alternate translation: [They will not worship idols at their altars] or [The people of Israel will not go to their altars and ask their idols to help them]

**Quote:** the work of their hands
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Here the people are represented by their “hands” to emphasize that they made the altars or idols. Alternate translation: [that they made with their hands] or [which they themselves built]

**Quote:** what their fingers have made … the Asherah poles or the sun images
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The second phrase identifies the things that the people made. Speaking of the fingers here emphasizes that the people made them, so these things are not real gods. Alternate translation: [the Asherah poles or the sun images, which they themselves made]

---

## Isaiah 17:9

**Quote:** that were forsaken because of the people of Israel
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
It can be made explicit who left these lands. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [that the Hivites and Amorites left after the people of Israel came] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

---

## Isaiah 17:10

**Quote:** For you have forgotten
Here “you” refers to the people of Israel. The word “forgotten” does not mean they have no memory of God. It means they no longer obey him. Alternate translation: [For you no longer obey]

**Quote:** the God of your salvation
Alternate translation: [the God who saves you]

**Quote:** have ignored the rock of your strength
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This compares God to a large rock which people could climb on to get away from their enemies or hide behind. Alternate translation: [have ignored God, who is like a rock that protects you] or [have ignored the one who protects you]

---

## Isaiah 17:11

**Quote:** the harvest will fail
Alternate translation: [there will not be much fruit for you to harvest]

---

## Isaiah 17:12

**Quote:** The uproar of many peoples, that roar like the roaring of the seas
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
An uproar is a very loud noise. Alternate translation: [The sound of many people, that is very loud like the seas]

**Quote:** the rushing of nations, that rush like the rushing of mighty waters
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
The enemy armies appear to be a powerful force that no one can stop. Alternate translation: [the nations come rushing in like the mighty waters]

**Quote:** the rushing of nations
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
The word “nations” refers to the armies of those nations. Alternate translation: [the rushing of the enemy armies]

---

## Isaiah 17:13

**Quote:** before the wind like chaff on the mountains … like weeds whirling before a storm
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean the same thing. The enemy armies seem powerful but God will easily stop them and send them away. Alternate translation: [like chaff on the mountains that the wind blows away … like weeds that whirl and blow away as a storm approaches] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

---

## Isaiah 17:14

**Quote:** This is the portion of those
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
What happens to them is spoken of as if it is a portion that they inherit. Alternate translation: [This is what happens to those]

**Quote:** loot us … rob us
The word “us” refers to Isaiah and the people of Judah.
