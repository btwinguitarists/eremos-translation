## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 60 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Israel’s restoration

This chapter pictures a time when Israel will be fully restored to Yahweh. They will be everything Yahweh intended them to be. All the nations will look to Israel and acknowledge her special relationship to Yahweh. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/restore]])

---

## Isaiah 60:1

**Quote:** Arise, shine; for your light has come, and the glory of Yahweh has risen on you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This refers to the glorious things Yahweh has done for the inhabitants of Jerusalem. They are now to show that glory through what they do and say and have hope in the future.

---

## Isaiah 60:2

**Quote:** Though darkness will cover the earth, and thick darkness the nations
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
Both of these phrases mean the same thing and are combined for emphasis. They refer to “spiritual darkness.” This means all the other people of the world will not know Yahweh or how to please him. This is a metaphor for divine judgment. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

**Quote:** yet Yahweh will arise upon you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This means the light of God’s presence will appear for the people of Israel, and it will show the way they should go.

**Quote:** his glory will be seen on you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [the people of the nations will see his glory on you]

## Isaiah 60:4

Here Yahweh begins speaking.

**Quote:** They all gather themselves
“They” refers to the rest of the people of Israel who will come together to return to Jerusalem.

**Quote:** your daughters will be carried in their arms
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [they will carry your daughters in their arms] or [they will carry your daughters on their hips]

## Isaiah 60:5

**Quote:** Then you will look and be radiant, and your heart shall rejoice and overflow
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These phrases share similar meanings and emphasize that they will be very happy because of what will happen in Jerusalem.

**Quote:** the abundance of the sea
This refers to the riches and goods that will come to Jerusalem by way of shipping, perhaps from along the Mediterranean Sea.

**Quote:** be poured out to you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This describes that the abundance will be like water that gushes out.

## Isaiah 60:8

**Quote:** Who are these who fly along like a cloud, and like the doves to their shelters?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses questions and poetic images to draw attention here. He compares the sails of the ships to clouds and doves. This is also a picture of the Israelites returning to the country where they belong. Alternate translation: [Look, I see something like clouds moving quickly and like doves returning to their shelters.] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

This expression usually means large merchant ships suitable for long voyages.

## Isaiah 60:17

**Quote:** instead of wood, bronze, and instead of stones, iron
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
Yahweh will give more valuable building materials to them now. Alternate translation: [instead of wood, I will bring bronze, and instead of stones, I will bring iron]

**Quote:** I will appoint peace as your governors, and justice your rulers
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
Yahweh describes “peace” and “justice” as human rulers. This means there will be complete peace and justice in the land of Israel.

## Isaiah 60:18

**Quote:** Violence will no longer be heard in your land
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive

**Quote:** but you will call your walls Salvation, and your gates Praise
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy

## Isaiah 60:20

**Quote:** for Yahweh will be your everlasting light
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
Both verses repeat this same idea for emphasis.

**Quote:** Your sun will no longer set, nor will your moon withdraw and disappear
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The moon will not literally disappear. This exaggeration is saying that the light of the sun and the moon will be much less bright than the light of Yahweh. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-hyperbole]])

## Isaiah 60:21

**Quote:** the branch of my planting, the work of my hands
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These phrases mean approximately the same and are repeated for emphasis.

**Quote:** the branch of my planting
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh is comparing the people to young shoots coming up that he planted as if he was a gardener. Yahweh has placed his people in the land of Israel. This gives hope to the people.

**Quote:** the work of my hands
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh compares the people of Israel to something made by the skill of his handicraft.

**Quote:** that I may be glorified
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [so that all people may glorify me]
