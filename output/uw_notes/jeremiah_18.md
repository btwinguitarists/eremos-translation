## Chapter intro

# Jeremiah 18 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in 18:13–17, 19–23.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Attacking Jeremiah

Things were so bad in Judah, they even attacked Jeremiah. To attack Yahweh’s prophet was to attack Yahweh himself. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Simile

Like a potter can change what he is making from one type of pot to another, God can change his plans and not punish people if they repent. The image of God as a potter and mankind as clay is an important one in Scripture. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/repent]] and [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

---

## Jeremiah 18:2

**Quote:** the potter’s house
“the workshop of the potter.” A potter is someone who makes pots from clay.

---

## Jeremiah 18:3

**Quote:** behold!
The word “behold” alerts us to a new person in the story. Your language may have a way of doing this.

**Quote:** on the potter’s wheel
The potter’s wheel is a small table that spins. A potter uses it to make pots.

---

## Jeremiah 18:4

**Quote:** the pot he was making from the clay was spoiled in the potter’s hand
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
The word “spoiled” means that something about the pot made the potter unhappy. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [the pot that the potter was making with his hands was not good]

**Quote:** was making
Alternate translation: [was forming] or “was shaping”

**Quote:** he returned the clay and formed it into another pot
He stopped making the pot, crushed the clay back into a lump, and then used that lump of clay to make another pot.

**Quote:** that was pleasing in his eyes
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “eyes” represents sight and sight represents one’s opinion. Alternate translation: [that he thought was good]

---

## Jeremiah 18:6

**Quote:** Should I not be able to act like this potter with you, house of Israel?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
With this question, Yahweh emphasizes his authority to do as he pleases with Israel. Alternate translation: [I am allowed to act toward you, house of Israel, like the potter acts toward the clay.]

**Quote:** house of Israel
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The word “house” is a metonym for the family that lives in the house. In this case it refers to the kingdom of Israel. See how you translated this in [Jeremiah 3:18](../03/18.md). Alternate translation: [Israel] or “kingdom of Israel” or “people of Israel”

**Quote:** Behold
Alternate translation: [Pay attention, because what I am about to say is both true and important]

**Quote:** Like clay in a potter’s hand—that is how you are in my hand
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
Yahweh compares his being able to remake Israel as he see fit to the way that the potter is able to remake a lump of clay as he sees fit.

---

## Jeremiah 18:7

**Quote:** tear it down, or destroy it
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
These two expressions basically mean the same thing.

---

## Jeremiah 18:8

**Quote:** relent from
Alternate translation: [withhold] or “not bring”

---

## Jeremiah 18:9

**Quote:** build it up or plant it
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
These two phrases mean basically the same thing.

---

## Jeremiah 18:10

**Quote:** if it does evil in my eyes
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “it” refers to a nation or kingdom, which is a metonym for the people in that nation or kingdom. The word “eyes” represents seeing, and seeing represents thoughts or judgment. Alternate translation: [if the people of that nation do things that I consider to be evil] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

**Quote:** not listening to my voice
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “voice” represents the words that Yahweh speaks. Here, “not listening” is an idiom that means that they do not obey. Alternate translation: [not obeying what I say] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom]])

---

## Jeremiah 18:11

**Quote:** See
This tells the reader to pay special attention to what follows. Alternate translation: [Look] or “Listen” or “Pay attention to what I am about to tell you”

**Quote:** I am about to form disaster against you. I am about to devise a plan against you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of planning disaster as if disaster were an object that he forms, like the potter would form clay. The second sentence repeats the same idea as the first using different words. Alternate translation: [I am about to devise a plan to bring disaster against you] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism]])

**Quote:** devise a plan
Alternate translation: [make a plan]

**Quote:** his wicked way
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of a person’s lifestyle as if it were a “way” or path along which the person walks. Alternate translation: [his wicked way of living]

**Quote:** so your ways and your practices will bring good to you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
The words “ways” and “practices” both refer to a person’s actions and general lifestyle. Alternate translation: [so your actions will result in good happening to you]

---

## Jeremiah 18:12

**Quote:** But they will say
The word “they” refers to the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem.

**Quote:** This is no use
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
Here, this idiom could mean: (1) the people do not care about what Jeremiah will say to them. Alternate translation: [We do not care what you say] or (2) The people do not think that anything good will come of what Jeremiah tells them to do. Alternate translation: [What you are saying will not work]

**Quote:** what his evil, stubborn heart desires
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “heart” represents the person’s mind or will. Alternate translation: [what his evil, stubborn mind desires] or “whatever evil things that we stubbornly want to do”

---

## Jeremiah 18:13

**Quote:** Ask the nations, who has ever heard of such a thing as this?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Here the word “nations” represents the people in the nations. Yahweh asks this question to scold the people of Judah. Alternate translation: [Ask the nations if any of them have ever heard of such a thing as this.] or “Nowhere on earth has anyone ever heard of such a thing like this.”

**Quote:** The virgin Israel has committed a horrible act
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
It was common to refer to nations as if they were women. Yahweh speaks of Israel being pure and faithful towards him as if she were a virgin and of Israel being unfaithful to him as if she had “committed a horrible act.” Alternate translation: [Israel, who is like a virgin, has done something horrible] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

---

## Jeremiah 18:14

**Quote:** Does the snow in Lebanon ever leave the rocky hills on its sides?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh asks this rhetorical question to emphasize that the snow on the mountains never melts. Alternate translation: [The snow in Lebanon never leaves the rocky hills on its sides.]

**Quote:** the rocky hills on its sides
Alternate translation: [the sides of the rocky hills]

**Quote:** Are the mountain streams coming from far away ever destroyed, those cold streams?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh asks this rhetorical question to emphasize that the mountain streams never cease to flow. He speaks of the streams drying up as if they were destroyed. Alternate translation: [Those cold mountain streams that come from far away never cease to flow.] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

---

## Jeremiah 18:15

**Quote:** been made to stumble in their paths
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of a person’s lifestyle as if it were a path along which he walks, and of living unfaithfully to him as if the person stumbled along the path. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [this caused them to stumble in their paths] or “it is like they stumbled while walking on a path” (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** they have left the ancient paths to walk lesser paths
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of a person’s lifestyle as if it were a path along which he walks. Here, “the ancient paths” represents the way that Yahweh had told their ancestors to live and “lesser paths” represents living unfaithfully to Yahweh.

---

## Jeremiah 18:16

**Quote:** will become a horror
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
The word “horror” may be translated with a verbal phrase. Alternate translation: [will become something that horrifies people]

**Quote:** an object of everlasting hissing
The word “hissing” refers to a sound that shows strong disapproval and can be translated with a verbal phrase. Alternate translation: [an object at which people will always hiss]

**Quote:** passes by her
Here the word “her” refers to “Their land.”

---

## Jeremiah 18:17

**Quote:** I will scatter them before their enemies like an eastern wind
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of causing the people to flee before their enemies as if he were an eastern wind that scatters dust and debris. Alternate translation: [I will be like an eastern wind and scatter them before their enemies] or “I will scatter them before their enemies like an eastern wind scatters dust and debris”

**Quote:** will scatter them
Here the word “them” refers to “my people.”

**Quote:** I will show them my back, and not my face
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-symaction
“I will turn my back toward them, and not my face.” Turning one’s back towards someone is a symbolic action that represents rejection and turning one’s face towards someone is a symbolic action that represents favor. Alternate translation: [I will reject them and not act favorably towards them]

---

## Jeremiah 18:18

**Quote:** let us make plots against Jeremiah
Alternate translation: [let us make plans to harm Jeremiah]

**Quote:** the law will never perish from the priests, or advice from the wise men, or words from the prophets
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The words “will never perish” can be supplied to each of these phrases. This can also be expressed in positive terms. Alternate translation: [the law will never perish from the priests, and advice will never perish from the wise men, and words will never perish from the prophets] or “the priests will always have the law, the wise men will always give advice, and the prophets will always speak”

**Quote:** words from the prophets
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
Here “words” represents messages that the prophets speak from Yahweh. Alternate translation: [words of Yahweh from the prophets]

**Quote:** attack him with our words
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The people speak of speaking harmful words against Jeremiah as if their words were weapons with which they attack him. Alternate translation: [say things that will harm him]

---

## Jeremiah 18:19

**Quote:** Pay attention to me
Here Jeremiah begins speaking to Yahweh.

---

## Jeremiah 18:20

**Quote:** Will disaster from them really be my reward for being good to them?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Jeremiah asks this question to emphasize that good actions should not be repaid with bad things. Alternate translation: [Disaster from them should not be my reward for being good to them.]

**Quote:** they have dug a pit for me
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Jeremiah speaks of his enemies planning to kill him as if they had dug a pit in which to trap him.

**Quote:** to cause your fury to turn away from them
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “fury” represents the punishment that Yahweh intended to inflict upon them.

---

## Jeremiah 18:21

**Quote:** give their children over to famine
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
The idiom “give over” means to place a person in the power of another person. Jeremiah speaks of “famine” as if it were a person who could have power over others. Alternate translation: [cause their children to die from famine] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification]])

**Quote:** give them to the hands of those who use the sword
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “hands” represents power. Alternate translation: [cause those who use the sword to have power over them] or “cause them to die in battle”

**Quote:** let their women become bereaved and widows
“let the children and husbands of their women die.” The word “bereaved” refers to a woman whose children have all died.

**Quote:** their men be killed
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The verb may be supplied from the previous phrase.

**Quote:** their young men killed by the sword in battle
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The verb may be supplied from the previous phrase. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [let people kill their young men with swords in battle] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

---

## Jeremiah 18:22

**Quote:** Let a distressed shout be heard
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
The phrase “a distressed shout” can be translated with a verbal phrase. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Let people hear a distressed shout] or “Let people hear others shout in distress”

**Quote:** For they have dug a pit to capture me and have hidden traps for my feet
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases share similar meanings. Jeremiah speaks of his enemies planning to kill him as if they had dug a pit for him to fall into and set traps in which to capture him. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

**Quote:** they have dug a pit
See how you translated this in [Jeremiah 18:20](../18/20.md).

**Quote:** have hidden traps for my feet
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Here the word “feet” represents Jeremiah. Alternate translation: [have hidden traps for me] or “have hidden traps for me to step on”

---

## Jeremiah 18:23

**Quote:** Do not wipe their sins away from you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
Jeremiah speaks of Yahweh forgiving his enemies’ sins as if someone had written their sins down and Yahweh would erase them. This phrase means the same thing as the previous phrase. Alternate translation: [Do not pardon their sins]

**Quote:** let them be overthrown before you
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [let people overthrow them in your presence]

**Quote:** in the time of your wrath
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
The word “wrath” can be translated with a verb. Alternate translation: [when you are still angry with them]
