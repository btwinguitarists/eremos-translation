## Chapter intro

# Jeremiah 31 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in 31:1–6, 8–22, 35–37.

The ULT sets [31:29](../31/29.md) farther to the right on the page than the rest of the text because it is an important quotation.

This chapter begins with a transition from the previous chapter. “At that time” is a reference to the day of restoration mentioned in [Jeremiah 30:24](../30/24.md). (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/restore]])
## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### The new covenant

This chapter is an important teaching regarding the new covenant. God will make a new covenant that will bring forgiveness of sins and a desire to obey God. Scholars disagree about whether this new covenant has begun. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/covenant]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/forgive]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/sin]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Israel

In this chapter, Jeremiah uses the term “Israel” in reference to the nation of Israel as a whole. He also uses it in reference to the northern kingdom of Israel and their people group.

## Jeremiah 31:2

**Quote:** The people who have survived the sword have found favor in the wilderness
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
The phrase “found favor” is an idiom. Alternate translation: [While the people who have survived the sword have been in the wilderness, I have had grace on them]

**Quote:** who have survived the sword
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The word “sword” is a metonym for war. Alternate translation: [who have survived the war]

**Quote:** wilderness; I will go out to give rest to Israel
Another possible meaning is “wilderness, where Israel went out to find rest.”

---

## Jeremiah 31:3

**Quote:** Yahweh appeared to me
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Jeremiah speaks of himself as if he were the people of Israel.

**Quote:** I have drawn you toward myself with covenant faithfulness
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
If your language does not use an abstract noun for the idea behind the word **faithfulness**, you can express the same idea with a verbal form such as “faithful” or “faithfully.” Alternate translation: [I have been faithful to my covenant and brought you near to me] or “I have faithfully loved you and brought you near to me”

## Jeremiah 31:4

**Quote:** I will build you up again so you will be built
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
Yahweh purposefully repeats the idea that he will build Israel to emphasize it. Alternate translation: [I will build you up again. Remember this: I will build you]

**Quote:** tambourines
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-unknown
musical instruments with heads like a drum that can be hit and with pieces of metal around their sides that sound when the instruments are shaken

## Jeremiah 31:7

**Quote:** Shout … Shout … Let praise be heard … Say
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-apostrophe
Yahweh is speaking in an apostrophe to all people of the world, so these verbs are plural.

**Quote:** the chief people of the nations
Alternate translation: [the most important people group of all the nations] or “the people group that is more important than any other”

**Quote:** Let praise be heard
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
This can be translated in active form. Alternate translation: [Cause everyone to hear your praise]

## Jeremiah 31:8

**Quote:** See
Alternate translation: [Look] or “Listen” or “Pay attention to what I am about to tell you”

**Quote:** to bring them
Alternate translation: [to bring the Israelite people]

## Jeremiah 31:9

**Quote:** I will lead them as they make their pleas
Yahweh will lead them and they will be praying to Yahweh. Some ancient versions read, “Yahweh will lead them and will comfort them.”

**Quote:** I will be a father to Israel, and Ephraim will be my firstborn
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Here “Ephraim” is another name for “Israel.” Alternate translation: [I will be like a father to the people of Israel, and they will be like my firstborn child]

**Quote:** my firstborn
The firstborn had special honor and responsibility.

## Jeremiah 31:10

**Quote:** Hear … Report
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-pronouns
Yahweh is speaking to the nations, so these verbs are plural.

**Quote:** The one who scattered Israel is gathering her up and keeping her
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-pronouns
Yahweh speaks of himself as if he were someone else. Alternate translation: [I caused my people Israel to be scattered among the nations, but now I am bringing them home and keeping them]

**Quote:** as a shepherd keeps his sheep
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
A shepherd cares for and protects his sheep, and Yahweh is promising to care for and protect the Israelites.

## Jeremiah 31:11

**Quote:** For Yahweh has ransomed Jacob and has redeemed him from the hand that was too strong for him
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean basically the same thing. Alternate translation: [For Yahweh has rescued the people of Israel from their enemy who was too strong for them]

---

## Jeremiah 31:12

**Quote:** on the heights of Zion
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
“on Zion, the high place” or “on Mount Zion.” Being on top of a hill is a metaphor for being happy. If your language associates mountain tops with sadness, it would be best to leave out the metaphor.

**Quote:** like a watered garden
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This means they will be strong and healthy, and they will prosper.

**Quote:** they will never again feel any more sorrow
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-hyperbole
The world “never” is a generalization. The Israelites will almost always feel joy.

---

## Jeremiah 31:13

**Quote:** I will change their mourning into celebration
The abstract nouns “mourning” and “celebration” can be translated as verbs. Alternate translation: [I will cause them to mourn no longer but instead to celebrate] or “I will make it so they are no longer sad but happy”

**Quote:** I will change
Alternate translation: [Yahweh will change]

---

## Jeremiah 31:14

**Quote:** I will saturate the lives of the priests in abundance
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
If your language does not use an abstract noun for the idea behind the word **abundance**, you can express the same idea with a verbal form such as “many good things.” The lives of the priests are spoken of as if they were a field that would become completely wet when much rain falls on it. Life is a metonym for the person. Alternate translation: [I will give the priests all the good things they would like] or “I will satisfy the priests with good things” (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]] and [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** this is Yahweh’s declaration
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-123person
Yahweh speaks of himself by name to express the certainty of what he is declaring. See how you translated this in [Jeremiah 1:8](../01/08.md). Alternate translation: [this is what Yahweh has declared] or “this is what I, Yahweh, have declared”

---

## Jeremiah 31:15

**Quote:** A voice is heard in Ramah
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
This can be translated in active form. Alternate translation: [I hear a voice in Ramah]

**Quote:** It is Rachel weeping for her children
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Rachel was the wife of Jacob/Israel and was the mother of the tribes of Joseph and Benjamin. Her name is a metonym for the women of Israel who are crying because the Babylonians have killed their children or taken them away.

**Quote:** She refuses to be comforted over them, for they live no longer
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
This can be translated in active form. Alternate translation: [She will not let anyone comfort her, for her children are dead] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

## Jeremiah 31:16

**Quote:** Hold your voice back from weeping and your eyes from tears
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
The voice and eyes are spoken of as if they were people whom Rachel needed to keep from moving. Alternate translation: [Stop weeping aloud and stop crying tears]

**Quote:** Hold your voice … your eyes … your work … your children
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-you
Yahweh is speaking to the Israelites as if he were speaking to Rachel ([Jeremiah 31:15](../31/15.md)), so all instances of “your” and the commands are singular. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-apostrophe]])

**Quote:** this is Yahweh’s declaration
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-123person
In both instances of this phrase, Yahweh speaks of himself by name to express the certainty of what he is declaring. See how you translated this in [Jeremiah 1:8](../01/08.md). Alternate translation: [this is what Yahweh has declared] or “this is what I, Yahweh, have declared”

## Jeremiah 31:17

**Quote:** your future … your descendants
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-you
Yahweh is speaking to the Israelites as if he were speaking to Rachel ([Jeremiah 31:15](../31/15.md)), so all instances of “your” and the commands are singular. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-apostrophe]])

## Jeremiah 31:18

**Quote:** I have certainly heard Ephraim sorrowing
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Ephraim was the ancestor of the largest tribe in Israel. His name is a metonym for the people of Israel. Alternate translation: [I have certainly heard the descendants of Ephraim sorrowing] or “I have certainly heard the people of Israel sorrowing”

**Quote:** You punished me, and I have been punished
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
The speaker repeats the phrase to show either that Yahweh punished him severely or that Ephraim learned what Yahweh wanted to teach him through punishing him. The words “I have been punished” can be translated in active form. Alternate translation: [You punished me. Yes, you punished me severely] or “You punished me, and I learned from you when you punished me” (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** I have been punished like an untrained calf
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
When people train calves, they strike them and otherwise cause them to suffer. Yahweh had caused the people of Israel to suffer.

**Quote:** punished
Another possible meaning is “disciplined.”

## Jeremiah 31:19

**Quote:** I slapped my thigh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
in grief. If people use a different phrase or action in your language to show that they are very sad, you may want to use it here. Alternate translation: [I rubbed my brow] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-symaction]])

**Quote:** I was ashamed and humiliated
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
The words “ashamed” and “humiliated” mean basically the same thing and intensify the idea of shame. Alternate translation: [I was completely ashamed]

## Jeremiah 31:21

**Quote:** Place road signs for yourself … Set up guideposts for yourself … Set your mind … you should take … Come back
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-pronouns

**Quote:** Come back, virgin Israel!
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor

## Jeremiah 31:22

**Quote:** How long will you waver, faithless daughter?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh is telling his people that he is becoming impatient with them because they are not obeying him. Alternate translation: [Do not hesitate to start obeying me.]

**Quote:** waver
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This could mean: (1) be unable to decide what to do or (2) often go off the correct path, a metaphor for disobeying Yahweh.

**Quote:** new on earth—a woman surrounds a strong man
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
This could mean: (1) the people will be so safe that women will not need anyone to protect them or (2) this is an idiom for something no one would ever expect. Alternate translation: [new on earth—something as strange as a woman protecting a man]

## Jeremiah 31:29

**Quote:** Fathers have eaten sour grapes, but the children’s teeth are dulled
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/writing-proverbs
These words are a proverb that Jeremiah heard people say when they were complaining that Yahweh was punishing children for the sins of their fathers.

**Quote:** sour grapes
This could mean: (1) grapes with too much acid in them or (2) grapes that are not ripe. If grapes are not part of your culture, you may want to use a general word for fruit.

**Quote:** teeth are dulled
Use the common words in your language for how people’s mouths feel when they eat sour or unripe fruit.

**Quote:** everyone who eats sour grapes, his teeth will be dulled
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/writing-proverbs
Jeremiah restates the proverb so that it now means people will suffer as a consequence of their own behaviors.
