## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 2 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in 2:2–22.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### The last days

See the discussion of the “last days” in the General Introduction to Isaiah.

### Idolatry

While the first prophecy of Isaiah concerned the lack of justice in Judah, this chapter focuses on the presence of idolatry in Judah. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/justice]])

## Isaiah 2:2

**Quote:** in the last days
Alternate translation: [in the future]

**Quote:** the mountain of Yahweh’s house will be established
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
This can be stated (1) as a description. Alternate translation: [The mountain of Yahweh’s house will stand] or (2) in active form. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will establish the mountain on which his temple is built]

**Quote:** as the highest of the mountains
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Isaiah speaks of importance as if it were physical height. Alternate translation: [the most important of the mountains] or [the most important place in the world]

**Quote:** it will be raised up above the hills
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Isaiah speaks of honor in metaphor as if it were physical height. This can be stated (1) in active form. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will honor it more than any other hill] or (2) as a metonym for the people who worship there. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will honor the people who worship there more than he honors any other people] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]] and [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** all the nations
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “nations” is a metonym for the people of those nations. Alternate translation: [people from all the nations]

**Quote:** will flow to it
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The people around the world going to the mountain of Yahweh is compared to how a river flows. This emphasizes that many people will come, not just a few people. Alternate translation: [will flow like a river to it] or [will go to it]

## Isaiah 2:3

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
# General Information:

Isaiah speaks to the people of Judah in the form of a poem.

**Quote:** Jacob, so he may teach … and we may walk
Another possible meaning is “Jacob. He will teach … and we will walk”

**Quote:** he may teach us some of his ways, and we may walk in his paths
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
The words “ways” and “paths” are metaphors for the way a person lives. If your language has only one word for the ground on which people walk, you may combine these phrases. Alternate translation: [he may teach us his will so that we may obey him] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

**Quote:** For out of Zion will go the law, and the word of Yahweh from Jerusalem
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These phrases mean the same thing. Isaiah was emphasizing that all nations will understand that the truth is found in Jerusalem. Alternate translation: [People in Zion will teach God’s law, and people in Jerusalem will teach the word of Yahweh]

**Quote:** For out of Zion will go the law
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
“For the law will go out from Zion.” Isaiah speaks as if the law were something like a river that moves without people doing anything. Alternate translation: [Those to whom they teach the law will go out from Zion] or [Yahweh will proclaim his law from Zion]

**Quote:** the word of Yahweh from Jerusalem
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
“the word of Yahweh will go out from Jerusalem.” Isaiah speaks as if the word of Yahweh were something like a river that moves without people doing anything. You can make clear the understood information. Alternate translation: [Those to whom they teach the word of Yahweh will go out from Jerusalem] or [Yahweh will proclaim his word from Jerusalem] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis]])

## Isaiah 2:4

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
# General Information:

Isaiah speaks to the people of Judah in the form of a poem.

**Quote:** He will judge
Alternate translation: [Yahweh will judge]

**Quote:** will render decisions
Alternate translation: [will solve disputes]

**Quote:** they will hammer their swords into plowshares, and their spears into pruning hooks
The people of the nations will turn their weapons of war into tools for farming.

**Quote:** swords … spears … sword
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
These words are synecdoches for weapons of any kind.

**Quote:** plowshares … pruning hooks
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
These words are synecdoches for tools of any kind that people use in peaceful activity.

**Quote:** they will hammer their swords into plowshares
“they will make their swords into tools for planting seeds.” A plowshare is a blade that people use to dig into soil so they can plant seeds there.

**Quote:** their spears into pruning hooks
“they will hammer their spears into pruning hooks” or “they will make their spears into tools for caring for plants.” A pruning hook is a knife that people use to cut unwanted branches off of plants.

**Quote:** nation will not lift up sword against nation
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
“no nation will lift up its sword against another nation.” The sword is a metonym for war. Alternate translation: [one nation will not fight wars against another nation]

**Quote:** nor will they train for war
“nor will they even train to fight wars.” The writer expects his reader to believe that those who fight wars train before they fight and that some who train do not fight.

## Isaiah 2:5

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
# General Information:

In [2:5](../02/05.md) Isaiah speaks to the people of Judah, and in [2:6](../02/06.md) he speaks to Yahweh. Both times he speaks in the form of a poem.

**Quote:** House of Jacob
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
“You descendants of Jacob.” The word “house” is a metonym for the people who live in the house, the family. Here “Jacob” represents the nation of Judah, but it would be best to use “Jacob” here.

**Quote:** come
a gentle encouragement to do what the speaker is about to tell the hearer to do

**Quote:** let us walk in the light of Yahweh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Isaiah speaks of people learning and then doing what Yahweh wants them to do as if they were walking at night with a lamp that Yahweh has provided so they can see the path. Alternate translation: [let us learn how Yahweh wants us to live and then live that way]

## Isaiah 2:6

**Quote:** For you have abandoned your people
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-pronouns
“For you have left your people” and do not care what happens to them. Here the word “you” refers to Yahweh and so is singular.

**Quote:** they are filled with customs from the east
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Isaiah speaks as if the people were containers who were full of something from the east. Possible meanings are that he speaks of: (1) the deeds that the eastern peoples do. Alternate translation: [they do all the time the evil things the people who live in lands east of Israel do] or (2) people, specifically those who claim to speak to dead people, who have come from the east to do evil deeds. Alternate translation: [many diviners have come from the east and now live there] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

**Quote:** they practice divination
they try to tell the future by looking at things like animal parts and leaves

**Quote:** they shake hands with sons of foreigners
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-symaction
Clasping hands together is the symbol of friendship and peace. Alternate translation: [they make peace and work together with people who are not from Israel]

## Isaiah 2:7

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
# General Information:

Isaiah speaks to Yahweh in the form of a poem.

**Quote:** Their land is full of silver and gold … their land also is full of horses
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Isaiah speaks as if the land were a container in which someone had placed silver, horses, and idols. The word “land” is a metonym for the people themselves, and the words “is full of” is a metaphor for the people possessing these items. Alternate translation: [They possess much silver and gold … they also possess many horses] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

## Isaiah 2:8

**Quote:** Their land also is full of idols
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Isaiah speaks as if the land were a container in which someone had placed silver, horses, and idols. The word “land” is a metonym for the people themselves, and the words “is full of” is a metaphor for the people possessing these items. Alternate translation: [They also possess many idols] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** the craftsmanship of their own hands, things that their own fingers have made
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean almost the same thing and emphasize that these are not real gods. If your language has no general word for something someone has made, you can combine these two phrases into one. Alternate translation: [things that they themselves have made]

**Quote:** craftsmanship of their own hands
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
The word “hands” is a synecdoche for the people themselves. Alternate translation: [their own craftsmanship] or [their own work] or [things they have made with their hands]

**Quote:** things that their own fingers have made
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
The word “fingers” is a synecdoche for the people themselves. Alternate translation: [things that they have made with their own fingers]

## Isaiah 2:9

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
# General Information:

In [2:9](../02/09.md) Isaiah finishes speaking to Yahweh. In 2:10–11 Isaiah speaks to the people of Judah. Both times he speaks in the form of a poem.

**Quote:** The people will be bowed down, and individuals will fall down
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Here being low to the ground represents people who are completely humiliated because they realize all they trusted in is worthless, and they can do nothing to help themselves. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [God will make the people ashamed, and they will realize that all they trusted in is worthless] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** The people
human beings, as opposed to animals

**Quote:** individuals
Alternate translation: [each person]

**Quote:** do not raise them up
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The words “raise them up” are a metaphor for Yahweh forgiving the people. Alternate translation: [do not forgive them]

## Isaiah 2:10

**Quote:** Go into the rocky places
Possible meanings are the people should go into: (1) caves on steep hillsides or (2) places where there are many large rocks among which to hide.

**Quote:** hide in the ground
Possible meanings are that the people should hide: (1) in natural holes in the ground or (2) in pits that they dig in the ground.

**Quote:** from the terror of Yahweh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
If your language does not use an abstract noun for the idea behind the word **terror**, you can express the same idea with the verb “terrifying.” Alternate translation: [to get away from Yahweh’s terrifying presence] or [from Yahweh because you will be extremely afraid of him]

**Quote:** the glory of his majesty
“the great beauty and power he has as king” or “his royal splendor.” See how you translated this in [Isaiah 2:10](../02/10.md).

## Isaiah 2:11

**Quote:** The lofty gaze of man will be brought low
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
“Yahweh will bring down the lofty gaze of man.” A man with a “lofty gaze” is looking above everyone to show them that he is better than they are. Here all people are guilty of thinking they are better than Yahweh, and the way they look at those who worship Yahweh, is a metonym for their pride. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will make ashamed all people because they think they are better than he is] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** the pride of men will be brought down
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
“The pride of men” here is a metonym for proud people, and being “brought down” is a metaphor for being humbled or ashamed. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will bring down proud men] or [Yahweh will make proud people ashamed of themselves] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]] and [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** Yahweh alone will be exalted
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [people will praise only Yahweh]

**Quote:** on that day
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
This is an idiom. Alternate translation: [on the day that Yahweh judges everyone]

## Isaiah 2:12

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
# General Information:

Isaiah speaks to the people of Judah in the form of a poem.

**Quote:** who is proud and raised up
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
One who is “raised up” is proud and considers himself better than other people. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [who is proud and who raises himself above other people] or [who is proud and thinks that he is better than other people] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

**Quote:** who is proud … who is arrogant
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
Someone who is arrogant speaks and acts as if he is better than other people. These two phrases have similar meanings and are used together to emphasize that Yahweh will punish them.

**Quote:** he will be brought down
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
“every proud person will be brought down.” If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will humble him]

## Isaiah 2:13

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The “day of Yahweh of Hosts” will be against the cedars and oaks. This could mean: (1) these trees are a metaphor for the proud people whom God will judge or (2) God will actually destroy these mighty trees.

## Isaiah 2:15

**Quote:** high tower … impregnable wall
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
These refer to things people would build around their cities so they could defend themselves against their enemies. They are a metaphor for the Israelites’ pride and belief that they had no need for Yahweh and could stand against any punishment Yahweh would mete out to them for their sins.

**Quote:** impregnable wall
Alternate translation: [wall that nothing can break down or go through]

## Isaiah 2:22

**Quote:** whose life-breath is in his nostrils
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
This describes how man is human and weak by emphasizing that he needs to breath to live. Alternate translation: [who is weak and will die] or [who needs the breath in his nose to live]

**Quote:** nostrils
the holes in the nose through which people breathe

**Quote:** for what does he amount to?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Isaiah uses a question to remind the people of something they should already know. If it would be helpful in your language, you could express this question as a statement. Alternate translation: [for man amounts to nothing!] or [for man is not worth anything!]
