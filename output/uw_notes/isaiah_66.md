## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 66 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in 66:1–17, 21–24.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### In the end

In the end, Yahweh will enact his perfect justice. This is the true hope of this world. The new heaven and the new earth are an important part of this. In the end, this will be the perfect creation of Yahweh and will last forever. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/justice]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/hope]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/eternity]])

## Isaiah 66:1

**Quote:** Heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh compares heaven to a throne and the earth to a footstool to emphasize how great he is.

**Quote:** Where then is the house you will build for me? Where is the place where I may rest?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses questions to emphasize that humans cannot build a place for him to dwell.

---

## Isaiah 66:2

**Quote:** My hand has made all these things
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Yahweh is represented by his hand which emphasizes his power and authority. Alternate translation: [I have made all these things]

**Quote:** this is Yahweh’s declaration
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-123person
Yahweh speaks of himself by name to express the certainty of what he is declaring. Translate as in [Isaiah 30:1](../30/01.md). Alternate translation: [this is what Yahweh has declared] or [this is what I, Yahweh, have declared]

**Quote:** the broken and contrite in spirit
Here “broken” and “contrite” refers to a person who is truly humble and who suffers for his faith.

---

## Isaiah 66:3

**Quote:** He who slaughters … also blesses wickedness
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These four clauses all describe different ways evil people act and come to the same meaning for emphasis.

**Quote:** They have chosen their own ways
Alternate translation: [They have chosen to do evil things that violate the ways of Yahweh]

## Isaiah 66:6

# General Information:

Yahweh describes the punishment for the hypocrisy of the worshipers.

**Quote:** A sound of battle tumult
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The sound represents the real fighting that is going on in the temple as Yahweh is carrying out the punishment.

---

## Isaiah 66:7

**Quote:** Before she goes into labor, she gives birth; before pain is upon her, she gave birth to a son
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks about Zion as if it were a woman who is about to give birth. Although Zion was destroyed and the people no longer lived there, Yahweh promises that without delay and with little effort an entire nation will come from her.

---

## Isaiah 66:8

**Quote:** Who has heard of such a thing? Who has seen such things? Will a land be born in one day? Can a nation be established in one moment?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses questions to emphasize how unique this event will be. The series of questions builds tension until Zion is finally mentioned.

## Isaiah 66:9

**Quote:** Do I bring a baby to the birth opening … born?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh uses questions to emphasize that he will not fail to fulfill his promises to the people of Jerusalem. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

## Isaiah 66:14

# General Information:

Isaiah is speaking to God’s faithful people.

**Quote:** your bones will sprout
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
“Bones” refers to the whole body.

**Quote:** will sprout like the tender grass
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
“Tender grass” grows fast and strong and compares to the health and vigor of God’s faithful people.

**Quote:** The hand of Yahweh will be made known to his servants
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
Here “hand” refers to his power. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will reveal his power to his servants] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

## Isaiah 66:15

**Quote:** coming with fire
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh’s appearances in the Old Testament are often accompanied by fire that represents Yahweh’s anger and judgment.

**Quote:** like the windstorm
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile

## Isaiah 66:24

# General Information:

Yahweh finishes speaking.

**Quote:** They will go out
Here “they” refers to all the people, the faithful Israelites and foreigners, who come to worship Yahweh.

**Quote:** the worms … and the fire
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
Both clauses describe the same idea to emphasize Yahweh’s punishment.

**Quote:** the worms that eat them
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The worms represent the horror of decay and rot that are Yahweh’s punishment on the wicked.

**Quote:** the fire that consumes
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Fire also represents Yahweh’s judgment.

**Quote:** will not be quenched
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-litotes
This can be expressed positively. Alternate translation: [will burn forever]

**Quote:** all flesh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This expression represents all created living beings that shrink from the dead.
