## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 61 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

The first part of this chapter is probably a prophecy about the coming Messiah and not Isaiah because this was different than the message Isaiah delivered. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/christ]])

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Restoration

This chapter prophesies concerning a future time of peace and restoration. It is unlikely to have been fulfilled in Isaiah’s day. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/restore]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/fulfill]])

## Isaiah 61:1

**Quote:** The Spirit of the Lord Yahweh is on me
“Spirit” here is the Holy Spirit of Yahweh who compels or motivates the person. See how you translated this in [Isaiah 11:2](../11/02.md) or in [Isaiah 42:1](../42/01.md).

**Quote:** the afflicted
This refers to poor people, those in great sorrow, or oppressed people who have problems they cannot overcome by themselves.

**Quote:** liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison for those who are bound
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean the same thing. They state that God will certainly give freedom to captives.

---

## Isaiah 61:2

**Quote:** the year of Yahweh’s favor, the day of vengeance
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Both of these phrases refer to the same span of time. “Year” and “day” are both specific examples that represent the greater whole. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism]])

**Quote:** year of Yahweh’s favor
Alternate translation: [time when Yahweh will act kindly toward his people]

---

## Isaiah 61:3

**Quote:** to give … to give
Isaiah repeats this for emphasis.

**Quote:** a turban
“a headdress” or “a beautiful head covering.” This is a long piece of cloth that is wrapped around the head.

**Quote:** oil of joy … a mantle of praise
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
People put oil on themselves and dressed in beautiful, long robes during times of celebration and joy.

**Quote:** in place of a spirit of dullness
Alternate translation: [in place of sadness] or [in place of mourning]

**Quote:** oaks of righteousness, the planting of Yahweh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This means Yahweh has caused the people to be strong and sturdy.

**Quote:** that he may be glorified
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [so that the people’s lives may glorify him]

## Isaiah 61:7

**Quote:** you will have double
This probably refers to a double portion of land.

**Quote:** they will rejoice over their share … they will … their land … will be theirs
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-123person
This still refers to the people of Israel. This can be stated in second person. Alternate translation: [you will rejoice over your share … you will … your land … will be yours]

---

## Isaiah 61:9

**Quote:** Their descendants will be known among the nations, and their offspring among the peoples
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two clauses mean the same thing. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [People from other nations will know their descendants] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** their offspring among the peoples
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
This clause assumes the same verb as the previous one. Alternate translation: [their offspring will be known among the peoples]

## Isaiah 61:10

**Quote:** I will greatly rejoice in Yahweh
“I” refers to the people of God speaking as one person whom Yahweh has restored.

**Quote:** clothed me with the garments of salvation; he has clothed me with the robe of righteousness
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The people of God speaking as one person now have salvation and righteousness as their distinct appearance visible to all. “Garments” and “robe” are clothing that everyone can see.

**Quote:** as a bridegroom adorns himself with a turban, and as a bride adorns herself with her jewels
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This comparison emphasizes that the speaker is extremely happy, joyful, celebrating.

**Quote:** turban
See how you translated this in [Isaiah 61:3](../61/03.md).

## Isaiah 61:11

**Quote:** For as the earth produces its sprouting plants, and as the garden makes its planting grow
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
This is saying the same thing in two ways. The fact that whatever God says he will do is certainly going to happen is compared to the fact that seeds sprout after planting. Alternate translation: [Just as seeds sown in a garden sprout from the soil and grow] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

**Quote:** righteousness and praise to sprout up
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This expression describes these virtues as growing from seed like plants do.
