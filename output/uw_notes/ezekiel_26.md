## Chapter intro

# Ezekiel 26 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

This chapter begins a section of prophecy against Tyre. The Babylonians will destroy Tyre. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]])

---

## Ezekiel 26:1

**Quote:** in the eleventh year
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-ordinal
“in year 11.” This means the eleventh year after King Jehoiachin was taken into exile in Babylon. Alternate translation: [in the eleventh year of the exile of King Jehoiachin] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

**Quote:** on the first day of the month
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-ordinal
“on day 1 of the month.” It is uncertain which month of the Hebrew calendar Ezekiel meant.

**Quote:** the word of Yahweh came
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
This is an idiom that is used to introduce something that God told his prophets or his people. See how you translated this in [Ezekiel 3:16](../03/16.md). Alternate translation: [Yahweh spoke this message] or [Yahweh spoke these words]

---

## Ezekiel 26:2

**Quote:** Son of man
God calls Ezekiel this to emphasize that Ezekiel is only a human being. God is eternal and powerful, but humans are not. See how you translated this in [Ezekiel 2:1](../02/01.md). Alternate translation: [Mortal person] or [Human]

**Quote:** Tyre has said against Jerusalem
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the city names “Tyre” and “Jerusalem” represent the people of those cities. Alternate translation: [the people of Tyre have said against the people of Jerusalem]

**Quote:** Aha!
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-exclamations
The word “Aha” is a sound people make when they discover something. Alternate translation: [Yes!] or [This is great!]

**Quote:** The gates of the people are broken
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The people of Tyre use these words to refer to Jerusalem as if it were a city gate through which traders from the surrounding nations pass. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Armies have broken down the gates of the people] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** She has turned to me
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
Here the word “she” refers to the word “gates” which have “turned” on their hinges to open to Tyre. This phrase means that Tyre has now replaced Jerusalem as the city through which the traders from many nations pass. Alternate translation: [The gateway has opened to me]

**Quote:** I will be filled up
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here to be “filled up” represents being prosperous. Alternate translation: [I will become prosperous]

**Quote:** she is ruined
Alternate translation: [Jerusalem is ruined]

Here the word “nations” refers to their armies. Alternate translation: [I will gather armies from many nations that will beat against you like the waves in the raging sea] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

**Quote:** I will sweep her dust away and make her like a bare rock
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
Yahweh speaks of how he will cause Tyre to be completely destroyed. Alternate translation: [I will cause the armies to completely destroy the city, and they will leave nothing there]

## Ezekiel 26:5

**Quote:** Connecting Statement:
# Connecting Statement:

Yahweh continues to give Ezekiel his message to Tyre.

**Quote:** She will become
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
The city of Tyre is spoken of here as if it were a woman. Alternate translation: [Tyre will become] or [It will become]

**Quote:** a place for nets to dry out in the midst of the sea
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Part of Tyre was an island. This expression is a metonym for the results of Tyre’s destruction. Alternate translation: [an empty island used for drying fishing nets] or [a deserted island where people dry out their fishing nets]

**Quote:** she will become plunder for the nations
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
“Plunder” means things that people steal or take by force. Here Tyre becoming plunder represents its wealth being carried off by other nations. Alternate translation: [the armies of the nations will take away every valuable thing from Tyre]

---

## Ezekiel 26:6

**Quote:** Her daughters who are in the fields will be slaughtered by the swords
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [The enemy armies will slaughter with swords her daughters who are in the fields]

**Quote:** Her daughters who are in the fields
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This could mean: (1) “Her daughters” were the young women of Tyre who were working in the fields or (2) “Her daughters” is a metonym for nearby towns and villages on the mainland that supported the main city of Tyre. Alternate translation: [Her daughter communities who are on the mainland]

**Quote:** know that I am Yahweh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
When Yahweh says that people will know that he is Yahweh, he is implying that they will know that he is the one true God who has supreme authority and power. See how you translated this in [Ezekiel 6:7](../06/07.md). Alternate translation: [understand that I am Yahweh, the one true God] or [realize that I, Yahweh, have supreme power and authority]

## Ezekiel 26:9

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
# General Information:

The words “he” and “his” in these verses refer to Nebuchadnezzar, the king of Babylon, and represent the soldiers in Nebuchadnezzar’s army doing these actions. The word “your” refers to the city of Tyre.

**Quote:** Connecting Statement:
# Connecting Statement:

Yahweh continues giving Ezekiel his message to Tyre.

**Quote:** He will place his battering rams to hit against your walls, and his tools will tear down your towers
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Although the battering rams and tools are spoken of as acting against the walls and towers of Tyre, they would have been used by the soldiers in Nebuchadnezzar’s army against the walls and towers.

**Quote:** battering rams
“Battering rams” are large trees or poles that many men in an army would pick up and hit against a wall or door so they could break it down and get inside. See how you translated this in [Ezekiel 4:2](../04/02.md).

**Quote:** tools
Weapons or instruments made of iron used to tear down the towers

## Ezekiel 26:12

# General Information:

The word “They” in these verses refers to the soldiers in the armies of Nebuchadnezzar. The words “your” and “you” refer to the city of Tyre.

**Quote:** Connecting Statement:
# Connecting Statement:

Yahweh continues giving Ezekiel his message to Tyre.

**Quote:** They will plunder your riches and loot your merchandise
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
The phrases “plunder your riches” and “loot your merchandise” mean about the same thing and are repeated for emphasis.

**Quote:** luxurious
expensive and comfortable

**Quote:** Your stones, your timber, and the rubble
These refer to what is left after the walls and homes are torn down.

**Quote:** into the waters
Alternate translation: [into the sea]

## Ezekiel 26:18

**Quote:** Now the coasts tremble on the day of your downfall. The islands in the sea are terrified, because you are no longer in your place
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two lines are similar in meaning and emphasize the response of the surrounding people to the destruction of Tyre.

**Quote:** Now the coasts tremble
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “the coasts” represents the people living there. Alternate translation: [Now the people of the coastlands tremble]

**Quote:** The islands in the sea are terrified
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “the islands in the sea” represents the people living there. Alternate translation: [The people of the islands are terrified]

**Quote:** you are no longer in your place
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
The destruction of Tyre is spoken of as if it was a person who had died. Alternate translation: [you have departed] or [you exist no more]

## Ezekiel 26:19

**Quote:** Connecting Statement:
# Connecting Statement:

Yahweh continues giving Ezekiel his message to Tyre.

**Quote:** When I make you
The word “you” here refers to the city of Tyre. In the original language “you” here is feminine singular.

**Quote:** When I make you a desolate city, like the other cities that are not inhabited
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
Here Tyre is compared to other cities that no longer had people living in them.

**Quote:** the deeps
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-nominaladj
Here “the deeps” means the deep waters of the sea. Alternate translation: [the deep waters]

## Ezekiel 26:20

**Quote:** then I will bring you down to the people of ancient times, like the others who have gone down into the pit; for I will make you live in the lowest realms of the earth as in ruins of ancient times
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
What Yahweh would do to Tyre is compared to people going to the grave and cities falling into ruin. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

**Quote:** the pit
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This refers to the grave. Because the grave was thought to be the entrance to the world of the dead, the pit also represents that world.

**Quote:** stand in the land of the living
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This represents being restored to the city’s former existence.
