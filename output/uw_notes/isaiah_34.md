## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 34 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Destruction

There are many images used in this chapter which describe destruction. Here each of these metaphors describes complete destruction. (See: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Prophecy

This chapter appears to prophesy about a time of restoration when the Messiah will reign. It pictures a time of great peace and harmony in the world. The translator does not need to add an explanation, but should try to maintain the tense of the original text, as a future or uncompleted action. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/restore]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/fulfill]])

## Isaiah 34:1

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
# General Information:

Yahweh is speaking in poetry.

**Quote:** The earth and all that fills it must listen, the world, and all things that come from it
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
Here the earth is spoken of as being required to listen to Yahweh to emphasize that it is under Yahweh’s authority. These two parallel phrases are metonyms for all the people who live in the world. Alternate translation: [In all places everywhere on earth, everyone must listen to what I say] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

**Quote:** the world, and all things that come from it
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
This is the second of two parallel phrases. The understood words may be supplied in this phrase. Alternate translation: [the world, and all things that come from it must listen]

## Isaiah 34:2

**Quote:** he has completely destroyed them, he has handed them over to the slaughter
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-pastforfuture
Often prophets speak of things that will happen in the future as if they have already happened. This emphasizes the event will certainly happen. Alternate translation: [he will completely destroy them, he will give them over to the slaughter]

## Isaiah 34:4

**Quote:** the sky will be rolled up like a scroll
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. This compares what Yahweh will do to the sky to a person rolling up a scroll. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will roll up the sky in the same way that a person rolls up a scroll] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

**Quote:** all their stars will fade away, as the leaf fades from off the vine, and as the overripe figs from the fig tree
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This emphasizes even the things in the sky that people thought would be there forever will fall as easily as a leaf. Alternate translation: [all the stars will fall from the sky like a leaf falls from a vine or a fig falls from a tree]

## Isaiah 34:5

**Quote:** when my sword will have drunk its fill in heaven
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh describes himself as a warrior bearing a sword. The phrase “drunk its fill” speaks of Yahweh’s sword as if it were a person who has eaten and become satisfied. Yahweh uses this imagery to emphasize that there will be a lot of destruction in heaven and to state its completion. Alternate translation: [when I am finished destroying things in heaven] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification]])

**Quote:** look
This word is used here to draw the listener’s attention and to have them imagine the things being said. Alternate translation: [listen] or [and then]

**Quote:** it will now come down on Edom, on the people I am setting apart for destruction
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The word “it” refers to Yahweh’s sword. This continues the metaphor about Yahweh destroying things with a sword. Alternate translation: [I will come to punish the people of Edom, the people whom I have set aside for me to destroy]

**Quote:** on Edom
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Edom refers to the people who live there. Alternate translation: [on the people of Edom]

## Isaiah 34:6

**Quote:** The sword of Yahweh is dripping with blood and covered with fat … of rams
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This speaks of Yahweh killing the people as if he were a priest sacrificing animals. He does this by describing the sword of a priest. Alternate translation: [Yahweh sacrifices them as a priest sacrifices animals, whose sword drips with the blood and fat of lambs, goats, and rams]

**Quote:** For Yahweh has a sacrifice in Bozrah and a great slaughter in the land of Edom
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
The words “sacrifice” and “slaughter” may be expressed here as verbs. Alternate translation: [For Yahweh will sacrifice many people in Bozrah and kill many people in the land of Edom]

**Quote:** Bozrah
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names
This is an important city in Edom.

## Isaiah 34:7

**Quote:** will fall
Alternate translation: [will die]

**Quote:** Their land will be drunk with blood
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
This describes the amount of blood that will soak into the ground by comparing the land to a drunk person. Alternate translation: [Their land will be soaked with blood]

**Quote:** their dust made fat with fatness
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
Here “dust” means the dirt on the ground. This describes the amount of fat that will soak into the dirt by comparing it to a person that has become fat from eating so much animal fat. Alternate translation: [the dirt will be full of the fat of the animals]

---

## Isaiah 34:8

**Quote:** it will be a day of vengeance for Yahweh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
Here “day” is an idiom for a point in time; it is not a literal “day.” Alternate translation: [it will be the time when Yahweh gets revenge]

**Quote:** he will pay them back for the cause of Zion
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
This means that he will take revenge on them for how they had previously waged war against the people of Jerusalem. Alternate translation: [he will give them the punishment they deserve for what they had done to the people of Zion]

---

## Isaiah 34:9

**Quote:** The streams of Edom will be turned into pitch … become burning pitch
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The water and land becoming useless for drinking or growing food because it is burnt and covered in pitch and sulfur is spoken of as if their streams and land will actually become pitch and sulfur. Alternate translation: [The streams in Edom will be full of pitch and the ground will be covered with burning sulfur and burning pitch]

**Quote:** her dust … her land
Alternate translation: [Edom’s dust … Edom’s land]

**Quote:** pitch
a thick, black substance that burns for a long time

## Isaiah 34:10

**Quote:** It will burn night and day
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism
This means all of the time. Alternate translation: [It will burn throughout the night and the day] or [It will burn constantly, all night and all day]

**Quote:** from generation to generation
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
The phrase “generation to generation” refers to all generations of people who will live in the future. See how you translated this phrase in [Isaiah 13:20](../13/20.md). Alternate translation: [forever]

## Isaiah 34:11

**Quote:** will live there
Alternate translation: [will live in the land of Edom]

**Quote:** owl
See how you translated this in [Isaiah 13:21](../13/21.md).

**Quote:** raven
This is a large black bird. It is difficult to identify some of the precise kinds of birds mentioned in this passage. However, they were all birds that preferred to live in places where there were no people, so they symbolize deserted places.

**Quote:** in it
“there.” This refers to Edom.

**Quote:** He will stretch over it the measuring line of ruin and the plumbline of destruction
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This speaks of Yahweh as if he were a careful builder as he causes destruction in Edom. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will measure that land carefully; he will measure it to decide where to cause ruin and destruction]

**Quote:** measuring line … plumbline
These are builders’ tools. See how you translated similar words in [Isaiah 28:17](../28/17.md).

## Isaiah 34:16

**Quote:** Search through the scroll of Yahweh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-possession
The phrase “the scroll of Yahweh” means that it contains the messages spoken by Yahweh. Alternate translation: [Read carefully what is written in this scroll that contains the messages of Yahweh]

**Quote:** not one of these
Alternate translation: [not one of the animals]

**Quote:** None will lack for a mate
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublenegatives
This can be written as a positive statement. Alternate translation: [Each animal will have a mate]

**Quote:** for his mouth has commanded it
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Yahweh is referred to by his “mouth” to emphasize what he has said. Alternate translation: [for Yahweh has commanded it]

## Isaiah 34:17

**Quote:** He has cast lots for their places
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This speaks of Yahweh deciding where to cause the animals to live as if he actually cast lots for their places. Alternate translation: [He has determined where they will live]

**Quote:** his hand has measured it out for them by a cord
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
This refers to the way that people measured things in biblical times. Alternate translation: [he has given the animals their places]

**Quote:** from generation to generation they will
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
The phrase “generation to generation” refers to all generations of people who will live in the future. See how you translated the phrase “from generation to generation” in [Isaiah 13:20](../13/20.md). Alternate translation: [forever they will] or [they will always]
