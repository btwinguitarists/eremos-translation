## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 16 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

This chapter is a continuation of the previous chapter and is a prophecy against the nation of Moab. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]])

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in 16:1–12.

---

## Isaiah 16:1

**Quote:** Send rams to the ruler of the land
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-symaction
The Moabites will send rams to the king of Judah so that he might protect them from the enemy army.

**Quote:** Selah
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names
This is the name of a city.

**Quote:** the daughter of Zion
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
The “daughter” of a city means the people of the city. See how you translated this in [Isaiah 1:8](../01/08.md). Alternate translation: [the people of Zion] or [the people who live in Zion]

---

## Isaiah 16:2

**Quote:** As wandering birds, as a scattered nest, so the women of Moab are at the fords of the Arnon River
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
All the people of Moab, which includes the women, are forced to flee from their homes. Alternate translation: [Like birds without a home, the women of Moab will flee across the river to another land]

**Quote:** As wandering birds, as a scattered nest
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
These two phrases mean basically the same thing.

---

## Isaiah 16:3

**Quote:** provide some shade like night in the middle of the day
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The heat of the middle of the day represents the Moabites’ suffering from their enemies, and shade represents protection from their enemies. Comparing the shade to night shows that they want strong protection. Alternate translation: [protect us completely from our enemies as a large shadow protects people from the hot sun] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

---

## Isaiah 16:4

**Quote:** Let them live among you, the refugees from Moab
“Allow the refugees from Moab to live with you.” Here “you” refers to the people of Judah.

**Quote:** be a hiding place for them from the destroyer
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Providing a hiding place is spoken of as being a hiding place. Alternate translation: [give them a place to hide from those who are trying to destroy them] or [hide them from those who want to destroy them]

---

## Isaiah 16:5

**Quote:** A throne will be established in covenant faithfulness
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “throne” refers to the power to rule as king. If your language does not use an abstract noun for the idea behind the word **faithfulness**, you can express the same idea with a verbal form such as “faithful.” If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will be faithful to the covenant and he will appoint a king] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns]] and [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** one from David’s tent will faithfully sit there
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “David’s tent” represents David’s family, including his descendants. Sitting on the throne represents ruling. Alternate translation: [a descendant of David will rule faithfully]

**Quote:** as he seeks justice
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Seeking justice represents wanting to do what is just.

---

## Isaiah 16:6

**Quote:** We have heard of Moab’s pride, his arrogance, his boasting, and his anger
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The words “Moab’s” and “his” refer to the people of Moab. Alternate translation: [We have heard that the people of Moab are proud and arrogant, boastful and angry]

**Quote:** We have heard
Possible meanings are that: (1) Isaiah is speaking and “We” refers to him and the people of Judah, or (2) God is speaking and “We” refers to God.

**Quote:** But his boastings are empty words
Alternate translation: [But what they say about themselves means nothing] or [But what they boast about is not true]

---

## Isaiah 16:7

**Quote:** So Moab wails for Moab—they all wail
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
“Moab” represents the people of Moab. “So all the people of Moab will cry out loud over what has happened to their cities”

**Quote:** for the raisin cakes of Kir Hareseth
Alternate translation: [because there are no raisin cakes in Kir Hareseth]

**Quote:** raisin cakes
The Hebrew word used here means either “raisin cakes” or “men.”

**Quote:** Kir Hareseth
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names
“Kir Hareseth” is the name of a city.

---

## Isaiah 16:8

**Quote:** Heshbon
Translate the name of this city as you did in [Isaiah 15:4](../15/04.md).

**Quote:** Sibmah … Jazer
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names
These are names of cities.

**Quote:** The rulers of the nations have trampled the choice vines
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The land of Moab was known for its vineyards. Here God describes the land of Moab as one large vineyard. This emphasizes that the rulers, which refers to the armies, completely destroyed everything in Moab. (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

---

## Isaiah 16:9

**Quote:** Indeed I will weep
In 16:9–10 the word “I” refers to Yahweh.

**Quote:** I will water you with my tears
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
God speaks of his deep sorrow for these places as if he would cry much and his many tears would fall on them. Alternate translation: [I will cry much for you]

**Quote:** Jazer … Sibmah
Translate the names of these cities as you did in [Isaiah 16:8](../16/08.md).

**Quote:** Heshbon … Elealeh
Translate the names of these cities as you did in [Isaiah 15:4](../15/04.md).

**Quote:** For on your fields of summer fruits and harvest I have ended the shouts of joy
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The “shouts of joy” represent people shouting for joy about the harvest of their fruit trees. Alternate translation: [Because of what I will do, you will no longer shout for joy when you harvest your fields of summer fruit]

---

## Isaiah 16:10

**Quote:** I have put an end to the shouts of the one who treads
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here “the shouts” refers to the joy of the people who tread the grapes to produce the wine. Alternate translation: [therefore the people who tread the grapes do not shout with joy]

---

## Isaiah 16:11

**Quote:** So my heart sighs like a harp for Moab
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
The phrase “my heart” represents Yahweh and his sad feelings. He compares his sigh to the sound of a sad song played on a harp. Alternate translation: [So I sigh like a sad song on a harp] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

**Quote:** Moab
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This refer to the people of Moab.

**Quote:** my inward being for Kir Hareseth
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The phrase “my inward being” represents Yahweh. The word “sighs” is understood from the previous phrase. See how you translated Kir Hareseth in [Isaiah 16:7](../16/07.md). Alternate translation: [my inward being sighs for Kir Hareseth] or [I am very sad for the people of Kir Hareseth]

**Quote:** Kir Hareseth
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This is a metonym for the people of the city. Alternate translation: [the people of Kir Hareseth]

---

## Isaiah 16:12

**Quote:** Moab … himself … his
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
All of these words refer to the people of Moab.

**Quote:** his prayers will accomplish nothing
Alternate translation: [his prayers will not be answered]

---

## Isaiah 16:13

**Quote:** This is the word
“This is the message.” This refers to all he said in [15:1](../15/01.md)–[16:12](../16/12.md).

**Quote:** concerning Moab
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The word “Moab” refers to the people of Moab.

---

## Isaiah 16:14

**Quote:** the glory of Moab will disappear
Alternate translation: [The country of Moab will no longer be glorious]
