## Chapter intro

# Ezekiel 17 General Notes

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Breaking allegiance

The king of Judah had vowed to obey Babylon but instead he followed Egypt. Therefore, God said he would not prosper. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/promise]])

## Translation Issues in This Chapter

### Metaphor

Judah is compared to a vine that grows one direction and then another. (See: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

---

## Ezekiel 17:1

# General Information:

Ezekiel reports a message that God gives him.

**Quote:** The word of Yahweh came
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
This is an idiom that is used to introduce something that God told his prophets or his people. See how you translated this in [Ezekiel 3:16](../03/16.md). Alternate translation: [Yahweh spoke this message] or [Yahweh spoke these words]

---

## Ezekiel 17:2

**Quote:** Son of man
God calls Ezekiel this to emphasize that Ezekiel is only a human being. God is eternal and powerful, but humans are not. Alternate translation: [Mortal person] or [Human]

**Quote:** present a riddle and speak a parable
Alternate translation: [give them a puzzle to think about] or [tell this story as an illustration]

**Quote:** the house of Israel
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The word “house” is a metonym for the family that lives in the house, in this case the Israelites, the descendants of Jacob over many years. See how you translated this in [Ezekiel 3:1](../03/01.md). Alternate translation: [the Israelite people group]

---

## Ezekiel 17:3

**Quote:** long pinions, full of feathers
“the ends of its wings were long and full of feathers.” The word “pinion” means the outer end of the wings.

**Quote:** that was multicolored
The eagle’s feathers were many different colors.

---

## Ezekiel 17:4

**Quote:** It broke off the tips of the branches
Alternate translation: [It broke off the highest part of the tree]

**Quote:** took them
Alternate translation: [took the top of the tree] or [took the branches]

**Quote:** he planted it in a city of merchants
“he planted it in a city that had many merchants.” A merchant is a person who sells things.

---

## Ezekiel 17:5

# General Information:

This is a continuation of the parable about the eagle.

**Quote:** He also took
The word “he” is the eagle in the parable.

**Quote:** fertile soil
Alternate translation: [good ground]

**Quote:** He placed it beside a large body of water
Alternate translation: [the eagle planted the seed in a place where there was a lot of water]

**Quote:** planted it … like a willow
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
Willow trees grow in places where there is a lot of water. If the eagle planted the seed like a willow, it means that he planted it in a place with a lot of water. Alternate translation: [and planted the seed like one would plant a willow tree by water]

---

## Ezekiel 17:6

**Quote:** Then it sprouted
Alternate translation: [Then the seed began to grow into a plant]

**Quote:** a spreading vine low to the ground
Alternate translation: [a vine that spreads out on the ground]

**Quote:** Its branches turned toward him
The branches of the vine turned toward the eagle. This means that they began to grow upward towards the sky.

**Quote:** its roots grew under it
This could mean: (1) “its roots grew under itself into the ground” or (2) “the roots of the vine grew under the eagle.”

**Quote:** So it became a vine
Alternate translation: [That is how the vine grew]

**Quote:** produced branches and sent out shoots
Alternate translation: [grew branches and spread its shoots]

## Ezekiel 17:7

# General Information:

The introductory “but” shows that a different eagle is being described other than the one before.

**Quote:** another great eagle
Alternate translation: [another large eagle]

**Quote:** Behold!
The word “behold” here alerts us to pay attention to the surprising information that follows.

**Quote:** This vine turned its roots toward the eagle
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
Yahweh speaks of the vine as if it were able to behave like a person and intentionally turned its roots toward the other eagle to receive nourishment from it. Alternate translation: [The roots of the vine grew toward the eagle]

**Quote:** from the place it had been planted so it would be watered
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [from the place that the first eagle had planted it so that it would have water]

## Ezekiel 17:9

**Quote:** Will it prosper?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh asks this as a leading question to emphasize the negative answer. If it would be helpful in your language, you could express this question as a statement. Alternate translation: [It will not prosper.]

**Quote:** Will it not be uprooted and stripped of its fruit so that it withers, and all its fresh growth will wither?
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-rquestion
Yahweh asks this rhetorical question to emphasize that the vine will certainly be judged. If it would be helpful in your language, you could express this question as a statement. It can also be stated in active form. Alternate translation: [Someone will pull up its roots and pluck out its fruit so all of its growth of leaves will wither away.] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** No strong arm or many people will be needed to pull it out
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
The phrase “strong arm” represents a strong person. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [There will be no need for a strong person or many people to pull it out] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

## Ezekiel 17:17

# General Information:

Yahweh speaking about the king of Jerusalem.

**Quote:** his mighty army and a great company of men
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
These two phrases mean basically the same thing and emphasize how large and strong Pharaoh’s army is.

**Quote:** will not help him
Here the word “him” refers to the king of Judah.

**Quote:** siege walls
this refers to a tower with ladders that can be placed next to a wall and allow soldiers to get over the wall and into the city.

**Quote:** to cut off many lives
Alternate translation: [to kill many people]

## Ezekiel 17:18

**Quote:** Behold, he reached out
Alternate translation: [He did something even worse than that: he reached out]

**Quote:** reached out with his hand to make a promise
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-symaction
This refers to taking hold of another person’s hand as a sign of friendship and agreement. Here it represents the covenant oath that he made with the king of Babylon.

## Ezekiel 17:20

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Yahweh speaks of enabling the enemy army to capture the king as if he were trapping the king in a net.

**Quote:** he will be caught in my hunting net
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
This can be translated in active form. Alternate translation: [I will catch him in my hunting net]

## Ezekiel 17:21

**Quote:** All of his refugees … armies will fall by the sword
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The word “fall” is a euphemism for “die.” Here the word “sword” is a metonym for soldiers who kill people with swords. Alternate translation: [Soldiers will kill all his refugees … armies]

**Quote:** the ones who remain will be scattered in every direction
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [the ones who remain alive will flee in every direction] or [the enemy soldiers will scatter in every directions the ones who remain alive]

**Quote:** know that I am Yahweh
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
When Yahweh says that people will know that he is Yahweh, he is implying that they will know that he is the one true God who has supreme authority and power. See how you translated this in [Ezekiel 6:7](../06/07.md). Alternate translation: [understand that I am Yahweh, the one true God] or [realize that I, Yahweh, have supreme power and authority]
