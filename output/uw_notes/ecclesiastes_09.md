## Chapter intro

# Ecclesiastes 9 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in [9:2](../09/02.md), 5–6, and 11–12.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Judgment

This chapter explains that there is one thing that awaits all people: judgment. When people die, they will all face Yahweh’s judgment. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/judge]])

## Ecclesiastes 9:1

**Quote:** I thought about all this in my mind
Alternate translation: [I thought very deeply about all this]

**Quote:** They are all in God’s hands
Here the word “they” refers to “the righteous and wise people” as well as “their deeds.”

**Quote:** in God’s hands
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “hands” refers to power and authority. Alternate translation: [under God’s control]

**Quote:** whether love or hate will come to someone
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
This speaks of “love” and “hate” as if they are people that may come to visit someone else. Alternate translation: [whether someone will experience love or hate]

## Ecclesiastes 9:2

**Quote:** righteous people and wicked
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism
This refers to all people, emphasizing the two opposites of righteous and wicked people.

**Quote:** wicked … the good … the clean and the unclean
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-nominaladj
All of these phrases refer to people. Alternate translation: [wicked people … good people … clean people and unclean people]

**Quote:** the clean and the unclean
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism
This refers to all people, emphasizing the two opposites of clean and unclean people.

**Quote:** the clean
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
A person who is acceptable for God’s purposes is spoken of as if the person were physically clean.

**Quote:** the unclean
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
A person who is not acceptable for God’s purposes is spoken of as if the person were physically unclean.

**Quote:** the one who sacrifices and the one who does not sacrifice
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism
This refers to all people, emphasizing the two opposites of those who sacrifice and those who do not.

**Quote:** As good people … so also will the sinner
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism
This refers to all people, emphasizing the two opposites of good people and sinners.

**Quote:** will the sinner … will the man who fears to make an oath
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
It is understood that this refers to people dying. Alternate translation: [the sinner will die … the man who fears to make an oath will die]

**Quote:** the one who swears … so also will the man who fears to make an oath
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-merism
This refers to all people, emphasizing the two opposites of those who swear oaths and those who do not.

**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Here the word “hearts” refers to the thoughts and emotions. Alternate translation: [Human beings are full of evil, and their thoughts are of madness]

**Quote:** madness
Alternate translation: [folly]

**Quote:** they go to the dead
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-nominaladj
The phrase “the dead” refers to dead people. Here dead people represent the place where people go after they die. Alternate translation: [they go to the place where dead people are] or [they die and go to the grave] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

## Ecclesiastes 9:4

**Quote:** the living
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-nominaladj
This refers to people who are alive. Alternate translation: [who are alive]

**Quote:** just as a living dog is better than a dead lion
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
A “dog” was considered a lowly animal while a lion was considered a noble animal. This speaks of it being better to be lowly and alive than to be considered noble and dead. Alternate translation: [It is better to be lowly like a dog and to be alive than to be noble like a lion and to be dead]

## Ecclesiastes 9:10

**Quote:** Whatever your hand finds to do
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche
Here a person is represented by his “hand” since a person often uses his hands to work. Alternate translation: [Whatever you are able to do]

**Quote:** there is no work or explanation or knowledge or wisdom
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
The nouns “work,” “explanation,” and “knowledge” can be expressed as verbs. Alternate translation: [the dead do not work or explain or know or have wisdom]

## Ecclesiastes 9:12

**Quote:** when his time will come
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-euphemism
This refers to when a person dies. Alternate translation: [when he will die] or [when the time of his death will come]

**Quote:** fish are caught … birds are caught … the children of human beings are ensnared
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile
This speaks of people dying when they do not expect it, in the same way that people catch animals and kill them when they do not expect it.

**Quote:** the children of human beings are ensnared by evil times
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Also, this speaks of people experiencing disaster and unfortunate times as if they were being imprisoned or trapped. Alternate translation: [evil times are coming upon the children of human beings] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor]])

**Quote:** that suddenly fall upon them
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
This is an idiom. Alternate translation: [at times when they do not expect them to happen] or [that suddenly happen to them]
