## Chapter intro

# Isaiah 13 General Notes

## Structure and Formatting

Some translations set each line of poetry farther to the right than the rest of the text to make it easier to read. The ULT does this with the poetry in this chapter.

## Religious and Cultural Concepts in This Chapter

### Prophecies

It is unclear who is addressed by these prophecies. At times, it appears to be the people of Judah, while at other times it seems to reference the last days. This ambiguity should remain in translation. (See: [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/prophet]] and [[rc://*/tw/dict/bible/kt/lastday]])

## Isaiah 13:1

**Quote:** about Babylon
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
The name of the city stands for the people of Babylon. Alternate translation: [about the people of Babylon]

**Quote:** Amoz
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names
Amoz was the father of Isaiah. See how you translated this in [Isaiah 1:1](../01/01.md).

---

## Isaiah 13:2

**Quote:** cry aloud to them
The word “them” refers to soldiers from another country.

**Quote:** the gates of the nobles
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This could mean: (1) “the gates of Babylon where the nobles live” or (2) “the gates of the nobles’ large houses”

**Quote:** the nobles
Alternate translation: [the respected people] or [the rulers]

---

## Isaiah 13:3

**Quote:** my holy ones
Alternate translation: [the ones I have set apart for myself] or [the army that I have set apart for myself]

**Quote:** I have called my mighty men to execute my anger
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
Executing God’s anger represents punishing people because of God’s anger. Alternate translation: [I have called my mighty soldiers to punish the people of Babylon because they have made me angry]

**Quote:** even my proudly exulting ones
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
“even those who proudly exult.” Why they proudly exult can be stated clearly. Alternate translation: [even my people who proudly exult because of the great things I do]

---

## Isaiah 13:4

**Quote:** The noise of a multitude in the mountains, as of many people
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The words “there is” are understood. The phrases “multitude” and “many people” mean the same thing. Alternate translation: [There is the noise of many people in the mountains] or [There is the noise of a great crowd of people in the mountains]

**Quote:** The noise of a tumult of the kingdoms like many nations gathered together
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The words “there is” are understood. The words “kingdoms” and “nations” here refer to the same thing. Alternate translation: [There is the noisy commotion of many kingdoms gathered together]

**Quote:** is mustering
Alternate translation: [is gathering]

---

## Isaiah 13:5

**Quote:** from way over the horizon
Alternate translation: [from places far beyond the horizon] or [from very distant places]

**Quote:** his instruments of judgment
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The soldiers that God is sending to attack Babylon are spoken of as if they were weapons. “Judgment” represents punishment because God has judged Babylon. Alternate translation: [the army that he will use to punish Babylon] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy]])

---

## Isaiah 13:6

**Quote:** Howl
cry out loud. The word is usually used of an animal making a sound because it is in pain.

**Quote:** the day of Yahweh is near
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Something that is about to happen soon is spoken of as if it is coming near. Alternate translation: [the day of Yahweh will happen soon]

**Quote:** it comes with destruction from the Almighty
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The word “it” refers to the day of Yahweh. “It comes with destruction” means that destruction will happen on that day. “Destruction from the Almighty” means that the Almighty will destroy them. Alternate translation: [on that day, the Almighty God will destroy them] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns]])

---

## Isaiah 13:7

**Quote:** all hands hang limp
This shows that all the people are very weak and unable to do anything.

**Quote:** every heart melts
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
People being terribly afraid is spoken of as if their hearts melt. Alternate translation: [everyone is terribly afraid]

---

## Isaiah 13:8

**Quote:** pain and agony will seize them
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-personification
People suddenly feeling terrible pain and sorrow is spoken of as if the pains and sorrows were people who grab them. Alternate translation: [they will suddenly feel terrible pain and agony]

**Quote:** like a woman in labor
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Being in labor represents giving birth to a baby. Alternate translation: [like a woman giving birth to a baby] or [like the pain of a woman who is giving birth to a baby]

**Quote:** their faces will be aflame
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Their faces being hot and red is spoken of as if they were burning. Possible reasons for their faces being hot are: (1) the people are very afraid or (2) the people feel ashamed or (3) the people cry. Alternate translation: [their faces will be hot and red]

---

## Isaiah 13:9

**Quote:** the day of Yahweh comes with cruel wrath and overflowing anger
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
The day coming with wrath and anger means that there will be wrath and anger on that day. The abstract nouns “wrath” and “anger” can be expressed with the adjectives “furious” and “angry.” Alternate translation: [on the day of Yahweh, he will be furious and extremely angry] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit]])

**Quote:** overflowing anger
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Anger here is spoken of as if the one who is angry is a container filled with anger. Overflowing anger means that he is extremely angry.

**Quote:** to make the land a desolation
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-abstractnouns
If your language does not use an abstract noun for the idea behind the word **desolation**, you can express the same idea with the verb “ruin.” Alternate translation: [to ruin the land]

---

## Isaiah 13:10

**Quote:** The stars of heaven and the constellations
Alternate translation: [The stars in the sky]

**Quote:** will not give their light
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
Giving light represents “shining.” Alternate translation: [will not shine]

**Quote:** The sun will be darkened
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Yahweh will darken the sun] or [The sun will be dark]

## Isaiah 13:11

**Quote:** Connecting Statement:
# Connecting Statement:

Yahweh continues to talk about what he will do on the day of Yahweh.

**Quote:** the world
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metonymy
This refers to people. Alternate translation: [the people of the world]

**Quote:** the wicked … the proud … the ruthless
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-nominaladj
These phrases refer to people who have these qualities. Alternate translation: [wicked people … proud people … ruthless people]

**Quote:** the ruthless
Alternate translation: [cruel people]

**Quote:** will bring down the arrogance of the ruthless
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-metaphor
Being low often represents being humble. Bringing down people’s arrogance represents making them humble. Alternate translation: [will humble the ruthless]

---

## Isaiah 13:12

**Quote:** I will make men more rare than fine gold
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
Why there will be so few people can be stated clearly. Alternate translation: [I will cause so many people to die that living people will be more rare than fine gold]

**Quote:** men more rare than fine gold … mankind harder to find than the pure gold of Ophir
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean basically the same thing.

**Quote:** pure gold of Ophir
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-names
Ophir was the name of a place where there was pure gold.

---

## Isaiah 13:14

**Quote:** Like a hunted gazelle or like a sheep with no shepherd
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-unknown
Yahweh speaks of the defenseless people of Israel as if they were a gazelle or a sheep that was being chased by hunters or wild animals. Alternate translation: [Like gazelles that run away swiftly when people hunt them, and like sheep that have no shepherd run away from wild animals] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-simile]])

**Quote:** gazelle
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-unknown
an animal that is similar to a deer. People hunt them, and wild animals sometimes attack and kill them.

**Quote:** like a sheep with no shepherd
Sheep that have no shepherd have no one to protect them from wild animals that attack and kill them.

## Isaiah 13:15

**Quote:** Every one who is found will be killed … every one who is captured will die by the sword
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean basically the same thing. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [The enemy will kill with the sword everyone they find] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

## Isaiah 13:16

**Quote:** Their infants also will be dashed in pieces
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [The enemy will also dash their infants to pieces] or [The enemy will beat their infants until they die]

**Quote:** before their eyes
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-explicit
Here the people are represented by their “eyes” to emphasize what they see. It can be stated clearly that the parents are unable to help their infants. Alternate translation: [in front of them] or [while their parents helplessly watch] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-synecdoche]])

**Quote:** Their houses will be plundered
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
This means that everything valuable will be stolen from their houses. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [The enemy will plunder the people’s houses] or [The enemy will steal everything valuable from the people’s houses]

**Quote:** their wives raped
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
This means that the enemies will attack the women and force them to have sex with them. The words “will be” are understood. This can also be expressed with an active form. Alternate translation: [their wives will be raped] or [their enemies will rape their wives] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

# Connecting Statement:

**Quote:** the most admired of kingdoms
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [the kingdom that people most admire]

**Quote:** Then Babylon … will be overthrown by God
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive
If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [Then God will destroy Babylon, the most admired of kingdoms, the splendor of Chaldean pride, as he destroyed Sodom and Gomorrah]

The abstract nouns “splendor” and “pride” can be translated with adjectives. The word “splendor” refers to the beautiful appearance of Babylon. Alternate translation: [the beautiful city that the Chaldeans are so proud of]

## Isaiah 13:20

**Quote:** It will not be inhabited or lived in
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-doublet
The two verbs mean basically the same thing. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [No one will live in it] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])

**Quote:** from generation to generation
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-idiom
The phrase “generation to generation” refers to all generations of people who will live in the future. Alternate translation: [for ever] or [ever again]

**Quote:** The Arab
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-genericnoun
This refers to Arab people in general, not one person. Alternate translation: [Arabs] or [Arab people]

## Isaiah 13:22

**Quote:** Hyenas
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-unknown
Hyenas are large wild animals that look like dogs and eat dead animals. Their loud cry sounds like a person laughing.

**Quote:** jackals in the beautiful palaces
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-ellipsis
The words “will cry” are understood. Alternate translation: [jackals will cry in the beautiful palaces]

**Quote:** jackals
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/translate-unknown
wild dogs

**Quote:** Her time is near, and her days will not be delayed
**Support:** rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-parallelism
These two phrases mean basically the same thing. “Her time” and “her days” both refer to the time that God has chosen for Babylon to be destroyed. If your language does not use the passive form in this way, you can state this in active form or in another way that is natural in your language. Alternate translation: [The time that all of this will happen to the people of Babylon is near, and nothing will stop it] (See also: [[rc://*/ta/man/translate/figs-activepassive]])
